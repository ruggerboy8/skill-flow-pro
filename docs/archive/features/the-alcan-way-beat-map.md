# The Alcan Way — Beat Map (Phase B draft)

*Created 2026-06-25. This is the bridge from raw resources to story. It is a
**draft for us to curate together**, not a final script. It selects, per journey
stage, the hospitality "hero" moves worth dramatizing, and for each captures: the
Pro Move, the visible moment, the dialogue, the patient-impact insight, and the
principle it embodies. Once we lock the heroes here, Phase C writes the final
copy and Phase D derives the art + staging.*

## The editorial filter (the rules that decide what's in)

Three rules decide which of the 326 Pro Moves earn a place in this experience:

1. **Hospitality, not table stakes.** We feature the above-and-beyond human
   moves, not the administrative baseline. In the data this maps cleanly to
   **domain**: the **Cultural** domain is the hospitality layer (Welcoming
   Presence, Trust Building, Emotional Intelligence), plus a few **Clinical
   comfort** moves (Tell-Show-Do) and **Case Acceptance** relational moves
   (Values-First Discovery, Establishing Credibility). We skip Clerical
   (forms/records), back-office Clinical (charting/imaging), and scheduling
   mechanics, except to acknowledge them as the baseline the hospitality sits on.
2. **Pediatric, not general.** The library contains two parallel sets. The
   **low-ID Alcan originals** (IDs ~1–197) are pediatric: they name "the
   guardian," "kiddo," "the child," "the play area." IDs **~231–296 / 320–328**
   are a **general rewrite** (British spelling, "patient" collapses the
   parent+child into one person) — we **exclude** these. The **4000-band** is the
   newer comprehensive **pediatric Doctor** set — we use it for Doctor beats.
3. **Visible and patient-facing.** It has to be something a family experiences.
   (This is also why the **Office Manager** is not a character: their Cultural
   moves are team-facing, behind the scenes. The OM is the invisible enabler, not
   a journey beat.)

## North Star, and how the principles thread

After this, a staff member can name the three principles, say what one means for
*their* role, answer "why Pro Moves?" with *the patient*, and want to share it.
The emotional core, straight from the principles doc: parents arrive **braced**,
and what they remember is **"I didn't have to worry."** Every insight should land
on some version of that.

The three principles are a **lens that surfaces**, not the skeleton. The skeleton
is the journey. The principles recur like this:

- **Own the First Moment** → each role's first moment: the greeting, the
  assistant's pickup, the doctor's entrance.
- **Master the Moves** → the exam mechanics: Tell-Show-Do, the warm handoff,
  asking before telling.
- **Be the Reason** → the small human moments and the personal goodbye: the
  blanket, "raise your hand to pause," the walk-out, the day-after call.

## The journey is out-and-back

Arrive → **check in (front)** → go deep to the **exam** → come forward →
**check out (front)** → leave. The exam is the deepest point and the turn. Camera
grammar mirrors it: push inward to the exam, pull back out to the goodbye. The
front-of-house is one space, visited twice (check-in and checkout).

## The Beat Map

Legend: ⭐ = recommended hero beat (becomes a scene). The other moves under each

### Stage 1 — Check-In  ·  *front-of-house*  ·  role: Front Desk

**The standard:** Family arrives braced. The front desk makes them feel welcomed
before they have a chance to feel anxious.

**Table stakes (acknowledge, don't dwell):** verify forms before changing status
[2]; complete check-in mechanics.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **B1 The Greeting** | [1] "I always stand up and greet every patient and their guardian with a smile" | Front desk **rises** from the chair, eye contact, smiles | "Welcome in, Jessica! And this must be Johnny." | The parent decides in this second whether they can relax or stay on guard. Standing up says "we're glad you're here" before a single form is touched. | Own the First Moment |
| ⭐ **B2 Preview the Doctor** | [15] "I always preview the patient's doctor by name, and comment specifically on that doctor's expertise" | Front desk speaks about the doctor with warmth | "You'll be seeing Dr. Patel. ### Stage 2 — Transition to Chair  ·  *front-of-house → hallway*  ·  Front Desk → Dental Assistant

**The standard:** The assistant comes *to* the family, personally. No name
shouted from a hallway.

**Table stakes:** front desk alerts the clinical team [4]; wait under 10 min [17].

| | Pro Move | The visible moment | Dialogue | Insight (seed) | Principle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **B3 The Handoff** | [20] "I always greet the patient by name, smile with eye contact, and introduce myself by name." | Assistant **walks into the lobby**, crouches to the child's level | "Hi Johnny! ### Stage 3 — Chair (Exam Room)  ·  *the deepest point, the turn*  ·  Dental Assistant + Doctor

**The standard:** The densest stage. Comfort, transparency, and shared

**Table stakes:** charting, imaging, the clinical work itself (the 4000-band
Doctor moves live here but are mostly back-end; we feature the relational ones).

| | Pro Move | The visible moment | Dialogue | Insight (seed) | Principle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **B4 Tell, Show, Do** | [22] "I always tell, show, do before performing any action on a patient." | Assistant holds up the tiny mirror, lets the child see/touch it | "See this little mirror? It's just like the one at home, but tiny. I'll be gentle." | Before anything goes in a kid's mouth, they know what it is, they've seen it, they've nodded. The fear drops because the surprise is gone. | Master the Moves |
| ⭐ **B5 You're in Control** | [61] "I tell the patient they can raise their hand to ask me to pause at any time" | Assistant shows the hand-raise; the child practices it | "If you ever need me to stop, just raise your hand and I'll pause right away." | Giving a child the power to pause hands them back control. Worry drops when you're not trapped. | Be the Reason |
| ⭐ **B6 The Warm Handoff** | [4027] RDA presents the family to the doctor in the room + [4030] doctor greets by name | Doctor enters; assistant presents the case **in front of the family**; doctor greets the child | "Dr. Alex, this is Johnny and his mom Jessica. When it happens in the room, they see a team; in the hallway, they wonder what they're not being told. | Master the Moves |
| ⭐ **B7 Ask Before Telling** | [4035] "I always ask parents what matters most to them about their child's dental care before discussing treatment" | Doctor turns to the parent in the corner and asks first | "Before we talk about the plan, what matters most to you about Johnny's care here?" | Asking before presenting changes the whole conversation. | · texture | [121] blanket before the procedure; [122] headset + show; [190]/[4039] confirm the plan together before leaving | Assistant tucks a blanket; doctor checks alignment | "Would you like a blanket while we wait?" / "How are you feeling about the plan?" | The reason isn't always dramatic. ## Final beat list — v2 (after story review, 2026-06-25)

A `design-visual-storyteller` review pressure-tested the ten-beat draft above.
The candidate tables stay as the source pool, but **this v2 list is the locked
sequence.** The arc now tracks one protagonist: the **parent** (braced →
"I didn't have to worry"), with the **child** (scared → safe) as the secondary
arc. The exam room is shaped as a real **peak** instead of a four-beat plateau,
and the front and back ends are rebuilt for the share test.

### What changed and why

- **Added the brace (Beat 1, The Arrival).** The whole arc is "braced → didn't
  have to worry," but the draft never dramatized the *braced*. Now the experience
  opens on it. This is the recognition beat that earns the share ("that's our
  lobby").
- **Added the dread (Beat 5, The Walk Back).** Hallway 1 had zero content despite
  being a beautiful dolly-forward. We now stage the child's fear spiking on the
  way to the back. This *setup* is what makes Tell-Show-Do actually pay off.
- **Merged Tell-Show-Do + You're in Control (Beat 6).** They were a matched pair
  ("here's what it is" / "and you can stop me") and back-to-back they blurred and
  forced a mid-action principle switch. One beat, two halves, both Master the Moves.
- **Named the peak (Beat 7, The Warm Handoff).** The deepest room of the
  out-and-back now coincides with the parent's guard hitting the floor, in public.
  The anti-pattern (the private hallway conference) is the sharpest "what aren't
  they telling me?" worry in the whole piece. This beat gets the most scroll room.
- **Promoted the blanket (Beat 9).** It's the *first appearance of Be the Reason*
  and the descent from the peak; it shouldn't be buried as texture.
- **Reframed the Walk-Out as "The Seam" (Beat 10).** It has no Pro Move, so we
  make that the point: the connective tissue between named moves is yours to own.
  Honest, thematically rich, and it builds the case for writing that Pro Move.
- **Rebuilt the ending for sharing.** The review now echoes the exact beats just
  watched (closes the loop), and a final **Send It** beat converts a moved viewer
  into a sender by pointing at a specific coworker. Sharing is the North Star's
  real test, and the draft only hoped for it.

Thirteen beats, but 1, 5, and 13 are short single-overlay beats, so it still

### Sharpened insight seeds (the ones the review flagged weak)

These move the insight off staff-virtue/abstraction and onto the parent's
lowered guard. Draft voice, no em dashes; final wording is Phase C.

- **Beat 4 (Handoff):** "Nobody shouted a name across the room. Someone came and
  knelt down. For a second, the kid wasn't a chart number."
- **Beat 7 (Warm Handoff) — protect this one:** "No private hallway conference.
  The family heard all of it. In the room, they see a team. In the hallway, they
  wonder what they're not being told."
- **Beat 8 (Ask Before Telling):** "The parent came in ready to defend what
  matters to them. They didn't have to. They were asked first."
- **Beat 10 (The Seam):** a closing warm handoff. The assistant summarizes the
  visit and names the next step, then asks for a Google review on the way out
  (planting the Beat 12 payoff). Insight: "The handoff between two people is
  exactly where families get dropped, or don't. Nobody let go of them. And the
  review they'll write later starts right here, with someone who earned the ask."
- **Beat 11 (Goodbye) — was the weakest:** "They came in braced. They're leaving
  with their kid waving. That's what they'll remember. Not the filling. The fact
  that they didn't have to worry."
- **Beat 13 (Send It):** recap the three principles in the fixed chapter-card
  treatment, then the role nudge ("Your first moment is happening tomorrow. Do
  you know which one it is?") and the share line ("You just pictured someone
  who's always the reason. Send it to them.").

## Decisions: resolved and remaining

**Resolved:**
- **Doctor = Dr. Patel** throughout (the "Dr. Alex" in the library scripts is a
  continuity break; Patel is who the parent is told about and who the review names).
- **Pediatric filter confirmed:** low-ID Alcan originals + 4000-band Doctor set;
  exclude the ~231–296/320–328 general rewrites.
- **Hero set:** the v2 sequence above, per the story review.

**Still needs you / the clinical director:**
1. **The Seam (Beat 10) — RESOLVED by intuition.** It is a closing warm handoff:
   summarize the visit + name the next step (book the next visit, or hand to the
   treatment coordinator) + ask for a Google review on the way out. The review ask
   maps to Pro Move [67]; the summarize-and-handoff half is best-practice intuited
   from the rest of the experience. A dedicated handoff Pro Move could still be
   commissioned for the library later.
2. **Final review text (Beat 12).** Pick/adapt a real Google review whose clauses
   rhyme with Beats 6, 7, 8, and 11 so it closes the loop.
3. **Clinical director:** sharpen insight copy now (Phase C) and have them review,
   or loop them in first?
