Explore My Craft: three concepts for the Pro Moves library

This page explores how a dental assistant might browse and study the full catalog of Pro Moves for their role, outside the weekly check-in ritual. Each concept takes a different navigation metaphor for the same underlying data, 4 domains holding about 16 competencies holding about 60 moves, with the person's own coach levels, confidence, and practice history woven in as encouragement rather than scorekeeping.

Concept A: Pocket Deck

Metaphor: a deck of flashcards in your scrub pocket
Optimizes for: the 30-second moment between patients. No navigation at all, the app deals you one move and your thumb does the rest. Tuning the deck once ("where I'm still building") makes every future open instantly useful, and the flip-to-study rhythm builds a light spaced-repetition habit. Costs: you never see the whole catalog at a glance, so it is weak when a coach says "go look at the narration moves," and it depends on people trusting the shuffle instead of choosing.

Why it works

Kids fear surprises more than sensations. When you name the feeling first, their brain files it as expected instead of alarming, and the whole visit gets easier for everyone in the room.

Concept B: The Craft Atlas

Metaphor: a map of your craft you drill into
Optimizes for: orientation and the coaching conversation. All 16 skill areas sit on one colorful map with your coach level painted onto each tile, so "go look at the narration moves" is one glance and one tap, and the quarterly evaluation has an exact mirror to come study in. Growth is visible: tiles change as levels change. Costs: a move is always two or three taps deep, and the entry screen asks for more reading than a between-patients moment allows. Browsing can feel like reviewing a report card if the tone slips even slightly.

Concept C: Shelves

Metaphor: a streaming library with search and curated rows
Optimizes for: meeting every mood at the door. Search is first-class, so a coach's phrase ("look at the narration moves") lands in two seconds. Curated shelves ("Keep going", "From your spring evaluation", "Say it out loud") turn the person's own history and eval into ready-made study paths, and the "up next" footer keeps a study session rolling like a playlist. Costs: the shelves are only as good as the curation logic behind them, and the domain-competency structure fades into the background, so people may never form a mental map of the whole craft.

Why it works

Parents leave a visit holding a lot. One clear next step, said warmly and once, is the difference between "we'll think about it" and an appointment on the books before they reach the door.
