# Alcan Hospitality Principles

## What This Is

The Hospitality Principles are three staff-facing commitments that define what excellent patient experience looks like at Alcan — and more importantly, *why* it matters.

They were built in response to a specific problem: when we asked staff what the point of Pro Moves was, the answers were about personal improvement and compliance. Nobody mentioned patients. The Hospitality Principles exist to fix that. They put the patient back at the center of why we do what we do.

The model comes from Southwest Airlines, where a similar framework was built by analyzing unprompted customer compliments. 60% of those responses included the phrase "I was able to" — meaning what people valued most was the feeling of being moved forward without friction. We ran the same exercise on our own Google reviews. The equivalent phrase for pediatric dental isn't about momentum. It's closer to "I didn't have to worry." Parents arrive braced for a hard experience. When they leave without having had to manage it themselves — when the staff handled it — that's what they remember and talk about.

These three principles are the articulation of that outcome.

---

## The Three Principles

### 1. Own the First Moment

Every visit begins with a moment that sets the tone for everything that follows. The front desk greeting, the handoff from check-in to the back, the first thing a hygienist says when she walks into a room. These aren't administrative steps. They're the moment a parent decides whether they can relax or need to stay on guard.

Owning that moment means being fully present and switched on from the first point of contact — not warmed up by mid-appointment. It means the parent feels welcomed before they've had a chance to feel anxious.

**What this asks of staff:** Know when your "first moment" is for your role, and treat it as the most important thing you do in a visit. For front desk, it's the greeting. For a hygienist, it's the room entry. For a dentist, it's the introduction. Don't let it happen on autopilot.

---

### 2. Master the Moves

Pro Moves aren't a compliance checklist. They're the mechanics of excellent patient care — the specific, practiced behaviors that make visits feel calm, clear, and well-run. When you do them well, parents don't have to intervene. Kids don't escalate. The visit flows.

The connection between Pro Moves and patient experience isn't abstract. Our reviews say it directly: parents notice when staff take their time, explain what's happening, and work with kids in a way that's age-appropriate and patient. Those are Pro Moves. Every one of them has a patient on the other side of it.

**What this asks of staff:** Connect the move to the outcome. When you're working on a Pro Move, ask yourself what it looks like from the chair — or from the parent waiting in the corner. That's who it's for.

---

### 3. Be the Reason

This one is personal. It's not about the team or the office. It's about you, specifically, being the person a parent thinks about when they sit down to write a review — or when they tell a friend where to take their kid — or when they decide to come back after a hard first visit.

Be the reason they didn't have to worry. Be the reason their kid left excited instead of scared. Be the reason they chose us, and keep choosing us.

The "reason" isn't always dramatic. Sometimes it's a blanket during a procedure. Sometimes it's letting a six-year-old touch the equipment before anything goes in their mouth. Sometimes it's catching that a parent looks overwhelmed and walking them through what just happened. Small things that land because someone was paying attention.

**What this asks of staff:** Take individual ownership of the patient outcome. Not the team's outcome. Yours. Ask yourself, at the end of a visit: was I someone's reason today?

---

## How These Work Together

Own the First Moment is about the entry point. Master the Moves is about the execution. Be the Reason is about the relationship.

Together they answer the question staff should be asking — not "did I do everything right?" but "did this family leave better than they arrived?"

## Rollout

The Hospitality Principles launch at the December Summit alongside two supporting artifacts:

**POV Videos** — AI-generated walkthroughs of the patient experience from the perspective of a child and a parent. Designed to build empathy by making the patient's journey visible to staff who spend their days on the other side of it.

**Printed Cards** — Each staff member receives a card with the three principles. A system name or acronym is in development.

Staff who achieve two consecutive quarters averaging a 3 or above across all domains with no 2s will receive a certification pin, also awarded at the summit.
