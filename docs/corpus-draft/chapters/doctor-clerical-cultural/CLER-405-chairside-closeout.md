# CLER-405 Chairside Closeout

**Domain:** Clerical | **Role:** Doctor | **Tagline:** [ALCAN] "Complete documentation before departure"

## Overview

Chairside Closeout is the doctor's discipline of finishing every piece of documentation before the patient leaves the chair: signing clinical notes, sending referrals, printing prescriptions, and logging sedation medications in real time. It applies to every Alcan doctor, on every patient encounter. The unifying idea is simple: nothing that belongs to this visit gets carried into the evening, the next day, or a backlog.

## Why it matters

[ALCAN] The framework is direct about the stakes. Timely documentation "ensures accuracy, prevents backlogs, and protects clinical integrity." Immediate referrals "reduce delays, confusion, and follow-up failures." Printing prescriptions at the chair "prevents missed medications and unnecessary callbacks." And real-time sedation logs are "critical for safety, compliance, and auditing."

[ALCAN] The Why It Matters block for signing notes (move 4017) expands on this: completing notes immediately after treatment ensures that details like diagnosis, treatment performed, anesthesia used, patient behavior, and post-operative instructions are recorded accurately and completely. Delaying documentation increases the risk of missing details, inaccuracies, or incomplete records. Signing before the patient leaves confirms the record is complete and finalized for the care team to reference.

[GENERAL] There is also a team-trust dimension: when the chart is reliably complete the moment the appointment ends, the front desk, treatment coordinator, and billing team can act on it immediately without chasing the doctor.

## The moves

### 4017 - I always sign my clinical notes at the chair before the patient leaves.

[ALCAN] Timely documentation ensures accuracy, prevents backlogs, and protects clinical integrity. It is essential for clinical accuracy, continuity of care, and legal protection (from the move's Why It Matters block).

Practical guidance, adapted from the What Good Looks Like block:

- [ALCAN] Complete and sign clinical notes before the patient leaves the chair.
- [ALCAN] Ensure documentation accurately reflects clinical findings, treatment performed, and patient interaction.
- [ALCAN] Notes are finalized by end of day.
- [ALCAN] Finalized notes support accurate billing, continuity of care, and strong legal documentation, and let the care team confidently rely on the chart being complete immediately after the appointment.

[ALCAN] Self-check questions from the move's Gut Check block: Did I complete my documentation while the details were still fresh? Does the note accurately reflect diagnosis, treatment, materials, anesthesia, and patient behavior? Did I electronically sign before the patient left the operatory? Am I letting documentation accumulate into end-of-day or next-day backlogs?

### 4018 - I always send patient referrals at the chair before the patient leaves.

[ALCAN] Immediate referrals reduce delays, confusion, and follow-up failures. The Why It Matters block adds: completing referrals at the chair ensures families understand the next steps, allows immediate support from the care team for scheduling or coordination, and lets the doctor verify referral accuracy while the case is in front of them.

Practical guidance, from the What Good Looks Like block:

- [ALCAN] Send referrals in real time while the patient is still present.
- [ALCAN] Ensure parents leave with a clear understanding of the next step in care.
- [ALCAN] Accurately reflect the clinical reason for consultation in the referral documentation.
- [ALCAN] Assist with scheduling or coordination immediately if needed.

### 4019 - I always print prescriptions at the chair before the patient leaves.

[ALCAN] This prevents missed medications and unnecessary callbacks. From the What Good Looks Like block: print the prescription during the visit, not after the patient is gone, and confirm the family leaves with what they need.

### 4020 - I always complete sedation medication logs at the time of sedation.

[ALCAN] Real-time documentation is critical for safety, compliance, and auditing. From the What Good Looks Like block: complete the med log immediately after administration, real-time, not end-of-day.

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

**Good looks like** (sourced above, per move): every note signed at the chair, every referral sent in real time, every prescription in the parent's hand at dismissal, every sedation log written the moment medication is given.

**Common pitfalls** [GENERAL, except where the Gut Check blocks say otherwise]:

- [ALCAN] Allowing documentation to accumulate into end-of-day or next-day backlogs (named directly in the 4017 Gut Check).
- [ALCAN] Leaving the referral as a task to complete later (named in the 4018 Gut Check).
- [GENERAL] Telling a family "we'll call it in later," which turns a 30-second chairside task into a callback loop.
- [GENERAL] Batching sedation log entries at the end of the day from memory, which defeats the safety and audit purpose of the log.
