# CLER-406 Follow Through and Review

**Domain:** Clerical | **Role:** Doctor | **Tagline:** [ALCAN] "Timely responses build trust"

## Overview

Follow Through and Review covers the doctor's administrative reliability beyond the operatory: staying in the clinical lane and out of financial estimating, verifying special consents before treatment begins, setting sedation-day expectations in advance, turning around team requests within 24 hours, and using the AI radiograph overlay as a diagnostic second pass. It applies to every Alcan doctor.

## Why it matters

[ALCAN] The framework's Why It Matters blocks make three connected arguments:

- **Lane clarity protects families.** When doctors estimate insurance coverage, it can lead to inaccurate statements, inconsistent financial information, and confusion for families. Separating clinical decision-making from financial coordination lets each team member operate in their area of expertise, and prevents doctors from unintentionally making financial commitments on behalf of the practice (move 200).
- **Documentation and expectation-setting prevent surprises.** Special-consent documentation protects the patient, family, and practice by preventing misunderstandings and providing legal and clinical proof that the parent was fully informed before treatment (move 201). Explaining the parental separation policy at treatment planning, not on sedation day, builds trust, predictability, and a smoother experience (move 202).
- **Responsiveness keeps care moving.** Delayed administrative requests create bottlenecks that directly affect case acceptance, treatment scheduling, insurance processing, parent communication and trust, and team workflow and morale. Responsiveness demonstrates professional reliability and partnership with the team (move 4021).

## The moves

### 200 - I always focus on diagnosing and recommending the best treatment plan for the child, and I defer detailed financial coordination to the practice's administrative team.

Practical guidance, from the What Good Looks Like block:

- [ALCAN] Explain the clinical rationale for treatment clearly.
- [ALCAN] Maintain general awareness of common coverage patterns without providing exact cost estimates. It is fine to note that silver crowns are typically covered and zirconia crowns are not, without quoting exact pricing.
- [ALCAN] Avoid stating something is "covered" unless verified by the administrative team.
- [ALCAN] Do not offer free treatment, discounts, or financial exceptions without approval from the clinical director or leadership team.

### 201 - I always ensure that all procedures requiring special consent have the correct consent form completed, signed, and documented in the patient chart before treatment begins.

Practical guidance, from the What Good Looks Like block:

- [ALCAN] Proactively identify procedures requiring special consent, and verify the correct form is in the chart before engaging with the parent.
- [ALCAN] Clearly explain the procedure's unique considerations, risks, and expected outcomes; get the signature before treatment starts; sign the consent yourself when required; and confirm the completed form is saved and visible in the chart.
- [ALCAN] The framework lists the procedures carrying additional consents: SDF, Pedi-wrap, Oral Sedation, GA, Biopsy, Mesiodens, Frenectomy, and the Pregnancy Test Waiver for oral sedations on patients who have begun menses (we ask parents whether their child has started her period; if so, the parent signs the waiver or the patient takes a pregnancy test).

### 202 - I always review our parental separation policy for oral sedation and general anesthesia during the treatment planning appointment so parents are not surprised on the day of treatment.

[ALCAN] From the Why It Matters block: parents assume they can stay with their child unless told otherwise. If the separation policy is first discussed on procedure day, families may feel surprised, anxious, or resistant. Explaining it in advance helps parents understand why separation is necessary for safety and lets the team maintain a focused, distraction-free clinical environment.

Practical guidance, from the What Good Looks Like block:

- [ALCAN] Review the policy during treatment planning, make sure parents know what to expect on the day, keep messaging consistent between the doctor, the consent forms, and the team, and frame the policy as protecting the child's safety.

### 4021 - I Always Complete Team and Administrative Requests Within 24 Hours

[ALCAN] From the move's description: this covers email- or staff-requested tasks such as insurance narratives, 22-point sheets, parent follow-ups, or other documentation requests, completed within 24 hours, with same-day completion the aim whenever possible.

Practical guidance, from the What Good Looks Like block:

- [ALCAN] Provide clear, thorough, actionable responses so the team can proceed without additional follow-up.
- [ALCAN] Maintain no backlog of outstanding team requests, so the team can confidently rely on the doctor for timely support in case acceptance, insurance processes, and parent communication.

### 4022 - I always review radiographs using the AI overlay to ensure nothing is overlooked.

[ALCAN] From the Why It Matters block: AI-assisted imaging improves diagnostic accuracy, consistency, and early detection, helping identify subtle findings that might otherwise be overlooked. The doctor remains responsible for the final diagnosis, using AI to enhance clinical expertise.

Practical guidance, from the What Good Looks Like block:

- [ALCAN] Review radiographs with the AI overlay during diagnostic evaluation; interpret AI findings with clinical judgment; treat AI as a support tool and safety net, never a replacement for expertise; and let families know advanced technology backs their child's care.

## Scripts and examples

**Deferring financials (move 200):** [ALCAN]
> "My job is to determine the best treatment plan to keep your child healthy and safe. Our financial team will walk through the details of insurance coverage and costs with you."
> "Insurance plans vary quite a bit, so I don't want to give you incorrect information. Our financial team will review your benefits and go through everything with you line by line."
> "The white zirconia crowns are not typically a covered insurance benefit, whereas silver crowns usually are. That said, many families still choose the white crowns, and they are actually a fairly affordable option if that's something you'd prefer. Our team can go through the exact costs with you."

**Special consent (move 201):** [ALCAN]
> "As we discussed, this procedure has some specific considerations. I want to ensure we review and document your understanding and consent for [Procedure Name]."
> "For [Procedure Name], it's important to understand [specific risk/outcome, e.g., 'Silver Diamine Fluoride will cause dark staining on the treated tooth']. We need you to sign this form to confirm we've discussed this."
> "Since [Patient Name] is over 10 and has begun menstruating, we need to address the possibility of pregnancy. You can either elect a urine test or sign this waiver form understanding the risks of sedation during pregnancy."

**Parental separation policy (move 202):** [ALCAN]
> "Because this is a sedation appointment and not a routine dental visit, we have parents remain in the waiting room so our team can focus completely on your child's safety and monitoring."
> "Parents can stay with their child while the oral sedation medicine is taking effect. Once we begin the procedure, we'll ask parents to wait in the waiting room so the team can fully focus on safety for the procedure and monitoring."
> "For anesthesia, parents are welcome to come back as the child is falling asleep. After that, you'll be asked to step out to the waiting room and you'll be brought back when we are done, before your child wakes up."

**AI overlay (move 4022):** [ALCAN]
> "We use AI-assisted imaging as an extra layer of review when we look at X-rays. It helps highlight areas we want to look at closely so we can be confident we aren't missing anything."
> "The technology helps flag areas of concern, but I always review the images myself and make the final diagnosis."

There is no script block for move 4021; it is an internal working habit rather than a parent conversation.

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

**Good looks like:** the sourced bullets under each move above.

**Common pitfalls** [ALCAN where drawn from the Gut Check blocks, otherwise GENERAL]:

- [ALCAN] Making specific promises about insurance coverage without verification, or offering discounts or free treatment without clinical director approval (200 Gut Check).
- [ALCAN] Starting treatment before confirming the signed consent form is actually saved and visible in the chart (201 Gut Check).
- [ALCAN] First mentioning the separation policy on the day of sedation (202 Why It Matters describes the fallout: surprise, anxiety, resistance).
- [ALCAN] Letting administrative requests accumulate into a backlog, or sending replies the team cannot act on without another round of clarification (4021 Gut Check).
- [ALCAN] Treating the AI overlay as either the diagnosis itself or as skippable, rather than as a second diagnostic pass reviewed with clinical judgment (4022 Gut Check).
