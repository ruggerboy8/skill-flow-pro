# CULT-407 Reliability & Professionalism

**Domain:** Cultural | **Role:** Doctor | **Tagline:** [ALCAN] "Consistent presence builds team trust"

## Overview

Reliability & Professionalism is about the doctor as a dependable presence: on time and positive at morning huddle, thoughtful about the ripple effects of calling in absent, respectful in giving and receiving feedback, and disciplined about leaving the clinical floor "clean" with nothing waiting on them. It applies to every Alcan doctor, and it leans on the reality that doctors hold a natural leadership role whether they seek it or not.

## Why it matters

[ALCAN] The framework's Why It Matters blocks share one theme: the doctor's behavior sets the weather for the whole practice.

- Morning huddle sets the tone, energy, and alignment for the entire clinical day, and because doctors hold a natural leadership role, their attitude and tone during huddle directly influence team morale (move 4023).
- Every absence affects multiple people and systems. Patients may have waited weeks or months; unexpected cancellations cause frustration, lost time, and delayed care, and frequent or last-minute absences erode trust with both patients and team (move 4024).
- Doctors set the tone for how communication happens in the practice. When feedback is calm, direct, and supportive, people feel safe asking questions and improving their skills; and assistants often notice things that can improve care, so doctors should be open to feedback from the team too (move 4025).
- The clinical floor runs on the doctor's availability. When the doctor steps away with loose ends, patients wait numbed, teams stall mid-procedure, and the schedule quietly falls behind, because everything doctor-dependent stops at once (move 4049).

## The moves

### 4023 - I always show up on time, prepared, and positive for morning huddle

[ALCAN] From the move's description: huddle is an opportunity to lead through presence, preparedness, and constructive engagement, helping the team anticipate the day's needs and maintain strong workflow.

Practical guidance, from the What Good Looks Like block:

- [ALCAN] Attend on time consistently; stay present and attentive without phone or task distractions.
- [ALCAN] Participate constructively; no negative or dismissive comments about patients or families.
- [ALCAN] Contribute to a positive, solution-oriented atmosphere that energizes the team for the day.

### 4024 - I always consider the trust impact on patients and my team when deciding to call in absent to work.

[ALCAN] Absences affect patients, schedules, and team morale. Reliability is a leadership expectation.

Practical guidance, from the What Good Looks Like block:

- [ALCAN] Run the internal gut-check questions before deciding to call out.
- [ALCAN] Limit absences to genuine illness, emergencies, or situations where practicing would pose a safety risk.
- [ALCAN] Communicate unavoidable absences as early as possible, and proactively plan for foreseeable disruptions, such as arranging backup childcare.

[ALCAN] The Gut Check block gives the actual questions: Did I consider the impact on patients who may have waited weeks or months? Am I genuinely unsafe or contagious, or could I still practice safely even if not feeling 100%? Have I communicated as early as possible? Have I planned ahead for predictable disruptions like childcare?

### 4025 - I Always Give and Receive Feedback with Respect

[ALCAN] From the move's description: feedback is part of working as a team; the goal is to help each other do our jobs well and keep the day running smoothly, while maintaining trust and respect.

Practical guidance, from the What Good Looks Like block:

- [ALCAN] Deliver feedback directly, respectfully, and with specificity, keeping a calm professional tone even when corrective.
- [ALCAN] Use "Pro Move" language to discuss expectations (see the first script below for what this sounds like in practice).
- [ALCAN] Be receptive to feedback from the team without becoming defensive, and end conversations with people feeling supported and clear on how to improve.

### 4049 - Before stepping away from the clinical floor, I always make sure current patients have been attended to, the team has what they need, and no exams, checks, or clinical needs are waiting on me.

[ALCAN] From the move's description: stepping away is fine; stepping away clean is the discipline. This move is flagged conditionally applicable in the framework.

Practical guidance, from the What Good Looks Like block:

- [ALCAN] Scan for waiting exams, hygiene checks, and anesthetized patients before leaving the floor.
- [ALCAN] Confirm the team has direction and materials for what happens while you are away.
- [ALCAN] Tell the team where you will be and when you will be back; time office work to genuine lulls, and return promptly when called.

## Scripts and examples

**Morning huddle contributions (move 4023):** [ALCAN]
> "Here's what we need to keep in mind for Patient X, they may need some extra support today."
> "I've confirmed the treatment plan for Patient Y, and we'll need [specific material/sedation] ready."
> "Great job preparing for the busy schedule today, team!"

**Feedback with Pro Move language (move 4025):** [ALCAN]
> "One of my doctor Pro Moves is that I sign the note at the end of the visit before I leave the chair. In order for me to do that, I need the note finished when we're done talking with the parent. I'm noticing that isn't always happening when we do exams together. What can I help with so we can make that easier?"
> "Thanks for having everything ready for that patient. That made the visit really smooth."
> "Hey, next time let's make sure the X-rays are up before I come in so we can keep things moving."

**Stepping away clean (move 4049):** [ALCAN]
> "Before I step into the office for a few minutes: is anything waiting on me? Any checks coming up?"
> "I'll be in the office for about ten minutes. Grab me the moment the hygiene check is ready."
> "Everyone taken care of? Anything you need from me before I step out?"

There is no script for move 4024; deciding whether to call in absent is an internal judgment, guided by the gut-check questions above.

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

**Good looks like:** the sourced bullets under each move above.

**Common pitfalls** [ALCAN where drawn from the Gut Check blocks, otherwise GENERAL]:

- [ALCAN] Being physically present at huddle but distracted or multitasking, or contributing negativity instead of alignment (4023 Gut Check).
- [ALCAN] Calling out when you could still practice safely, or communicating an absence late and leaving the team to absorb the disruption (4024 Gut Check).
- [ALCAN] Venting frustration dressed up as feedback, or delivering feedback in a way that would embarrass rather than support the recipient (4025 Gut Check asks: "If I were in their position, would I feel supported or embarrassed?").
- [ALCAN] Finding out after you left the floor that someone was waiting on you, or stepping away during a busy stretch and creating the lull yourself (4049 Gut Check).

## FAQ seeds

1. **What does "prepared" for morning huddle actually mean for a doctor?** [ALCAN] The scripts show it: knowing which patients may need extra support, having treatment plans confirmed, and knowing what materials or sedation will be needed, so the team can get ready.
2. **I'm feeling under the weather but probably functional. Should I come in?** [ALCAN] The framework's gut check: limit absences to genuine illness, emergencies, or safety risk. Ask yourself whether you are genuinely unsafe or contagious, or could still practice safely at less than 100%.
3. **How do I raise a recurring problem with an RDA without souring the relationship?** [ALCAN] Use Pro Move language: name the shared expectation, describe what you are noticing without blame, and ask "What can I help with so we can make that easier?"
4. **Is it ever OK to step off the floor mid-day?** [ALCAN] Yes. The move says stepping away is fine and necessary; the discipline is stepping away clean: nothing waiting on you, the team briefed, and your location and return time known.
