# CULT-409 Family Presence & Communication

**Domain:** Cultural | **Role:** Doctor | **Tagline:** [ALCAN] "Calm presence builds patient trust"

## Overview

Family Presence & Communication covers how the doctor shows up to families in the room: a warm named greeting, a calm and confident presence, authentic conversation and storytelling instead of clinical lecture, and an explicit alignment check ("how are you feeling about the plan as it stands?") before leaving the room. It applies to every Alcan doctor, at every patient interaction.

## Why it matters

[ALCAN] The framework's rationale, from the Why It Matters blocks:

- The first few seconds of the interaction set the tone for the entire visit. A warm greeting with eye contact helps families feel seen, welcomed, and safe, and clearly stating your name eliminates confusion for new families (move 4030).
- Parents often evaluate a doctor's confidence and presence before they fully process the information being shared. A calm, grounded presence helps families feel safe and reassured, and improves parent trust, understanding of the treatment plan, comfort for the child, and overall case acceptance (move 4032).
- Pediatric dentistry thrives on trust, comfort, and effective communication. Storytelling and conversational language translate complex clinical concepts into real-world meaning, building rapport, improving treatment understanding, increasing case acceptance, and reducing anxiety for both children and parents (move 194).
- Diagnosis is the doctor's job, but the treatment plan is a shared decision. Closing the loop with an emotional alignment check ensures parents feel heard and understood, fostering trust and increasing the likelihood of treatment acceptance and follow-through (move 190).

## The moves

### 4030 - I Always Warmly Greet the Family and Introduce Myself by Name

[ALCAN] From the description: pause, make eye contact, warmly greet the child and parent, and introduce yourself by name when entering the room, especially at first meetings, and acknowledge and thank the RDA for the introduction to reinforce teamwork in front of the family.

Practical guidance, from the What Good Looks Like block:

- [ALCAN] Pause, face the family, and make eye contact before beginning the exam; greet both child and parent warmly; introduce yourself by name, especially on first visits; thank or acknowledge the RDA; keep the greeting calm, present, and unhurried.

### 4032 - I Always Project a Calm and Confident Presence

[ALCAN] From the description: parents and children often mirror the doctor's emotional state; calm, aligned presence supports comprehension and trust.

Practical guidance, from the What Good Looks Like block:

- [ALCAN] Speak with a calm, steady tone and controlled pace; use open, relaxed, intentional body language without fidgeting; maintain consistent, reassuring eye contact; pause thoughtfully rather than filling space with filler words.

[ALCAN] The Why It Matters block adds a self-awareness note: habits like rushing speech, filler words, fidgeting, or appearing uncertain can unintentionally signal discomfort or lack of confidence. Slowing down, holding eye contact, and speaking clearly create a more reassuring presence.

### 194 - I always use authentic conversation and storytelling with relatable examples to build trust with patients and parents.

[ALCAN] From the description: communicate in a way that feels human, relatable, and authentic, using clear explanations, conversational language, and appropriate storytelling, rather than delivering information in a purely clinical or transactional way.

Practical guidance, from the What Good Looks Like block:

- [ALCAN] Communicate findings in clear, relatable language rather than dense clinical terminology; keep conversations natural, engaging, and personalized rather than scripted or transactional; encourage parents to ask questions and participate in decisions.

### 190 - I always check to confirm parent alignment with the mutually agreed upon treatment recommendation before leaving the room, directly asking them "how are you feeling about the plan as it stands?"

[ALCAN] From the description: after reviewing treatment recommendations, pause to check how the parent is feeling about the plan so they feel heard and comfortable moving forward. This brief alignment check reinforces shared decision-making, builds trust, and helps prevent confusion or hesitation about next steps.

[ALCAN] The Why It Matters block frames the philosophy: "We do not dictate treatment to parents; this is a shared decision-making process."

## Scripts and examples

**Greeting (move 4030):** [ALCAN]
> "Thank you, Jenni. Hi Sarah, hi Mrs. Smith, I'm Dr. Alex. I'm so glad to see you today."
> "Hi there! I'm Dr. Alex. It's nice to meet you both."

**Calm and confident presence (move 4032):** [ALCAN]
> "I'm going to explain exactly what we'll be doing today so you feel comfortable and informed."
> "I sense you may have some concerns, and I want to make sure we address all of your questions."
> "Take your time, I'm here to listen."
> "It's completely understandable to feel worried. Our goal is to take great care of your child and help you feel comfortable with the plan."

**Storytelling and analogies (move 194):** [ALCAN]
> "Think of the enamel like the shell of an egg. Once the cavity gets through that outer layer, the inside of the tooth is softer and the cavity can grow faster."
> "I see this pretty commonly in kids this age. Those back teeth have deep grooves that love to trap snacks, and sometimes the toothbrush just can't reach them well."
> "A lot of parents feel surprised when cavities show up between teeth because they look perfectly normal on the outside. That's exactly why we use X-rays to catch them early."
> "What questions do you have about what we're seeing today?"

**Alignment check (move 190):** [ALCAN]
> "How do you feel about what we've discussed today regarding [child's name]'s treatment?"
> "What are your initial thoughts or concerns after hearing these recommendations?"
> "Do you feel comfortable with the proposed plan, and do you have any questions or anything you'd like to explore further?"

(Note: "Dr. Alex," "Jenni," "Sarah," and "Mrs. Smith" are example names used in the framework's scripts.)

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

**Good looks like:** beyond the bullets above, the framework describes the target outcome for the alignment check (move 190 What Good Looks Like): [ALCAN] the parent openly shares feelings and asks clarifying questions without hesitation, verbally acknowledges understanding, actively participates and offers their perspective, and expresses gratitude for having their feelings heard and considered.

**Common pitfalls** [ALCAN where drawn from the Gut Check blocks, otherwise GENERAL]:

- [ALCAN] Starting the exam without pausing to fully greet the family or make eye contact with both parent and child (4030 Gut Check).
- [ALCAN] Rushing speech, filler words ("um," "I guess," "I think"), fidgeting, or otherwise signaling uncertainty during difficult conversations (4032 Gut Check).
- [ALCAN] Delivering information in a purely clinical or transactional way instead of inviting the parent into the conversation (194 description and Gut Check).
- [ALCAN] Leaving the room without giving the parent ample opportunity to express feelings, or leaving emotional cues unaddressed that might hinder treatment acceptance (190 Gut Check).
