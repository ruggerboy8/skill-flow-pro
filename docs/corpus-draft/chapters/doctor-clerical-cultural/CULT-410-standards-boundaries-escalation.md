# CULT-410 Standards, Boundaries & Escalation

**Domain:** Cultural | **Role:** Doctor | **Tagline:** [ALCAN] "Empathy without compromising care"

## Overview

Standards, Boundaries & Escalation is about holding the line kindly. Three moves cover it: leading with "tell me more" when a parent hesitates on treatment, escalating promptly to leadership when a team or system issue blocks the standard of care, and meeting a parent's decline of treatment with a neutral restatement plus a curious question rather than pressure. It applies to every Alcan doctor.

## Why it matters

[ALCAN] From the framework's Why It Matters blocks:

- Parents may hesitate for many reasons, including fear, cost, or misunderstandings. Approaching these conversations with curiosity fosters a sense of being heard, leading to better understanding and alignment, and ultimately improving patient safety and accurate, proactive care (move 4033).
- Doctors should not feel they must solve persistent operational or staffing issues alone. When concerns go unspoken, teams may begin to normalize workarounds that lower standards, affecting patient care, team morale, and the doctor's ability to practice safely. Escalating early lets the organization identify patterns, provide support, and improve systems (move 4034).
- A decline met with pressure ends the conversation; a decline met with curiosity keeps the relationship. The open question usually surfaces the real driver: fear, cost, a past experience, or a misunderstanding, and most of those are addressable once spoken aloud (move 4052).

## The moves

### 4033 - I Always Ask "Tell Me More" When a Parent Hesitates on Treatment

[ALCAN] From the description: begin with curiosity and listening, seeking to understand concerns before responding. If concerns persist after discussion, clearly explain the practice's standards of care and clinical recommendations, and establish clear, respectful boundaries when a parent's requests repeatedly fall outside those standards.

Practical guidance, from the What Good Looks Like block:

- [ALCAN] Begin hesitant conversations with listening and curiosity; explain clinical findings and risks in understandable language.
- [ALCAN] Introduce standards of care only when needed to clarify boundaries, and establish clear respectful boundaries when requests fall outside the practice's standard of care.
- [ALCAN] Document recommendations and declined treatment clearly, and professionally support a transition of care if appropriate when care remains misaligned.

### 4034 - I always promptly loop in my office manager, regional manager, or clinical director when a team member or system issue prevents me from practicing according to our standards of care.

Practical guidance, from the What Good Looks Like block:

- [ALCAN] Flag concerns early when systems or staffing interfere with clinical standards; loop in appropriate leadership rather than attempting to manage chronic issues alone.
- [ALCAN] Keep the communication professional and solution-oriented, focused on patient care, and stay committed to standards of care even when operational challenges arise.

[ALCAN] The Gut Check block sharpens the trigger: Am I trying to solve a recurring issue on my own? Is this preventing me from practicing to our standard of care? Am I normalizing a temporary workaround that could become the new standard?

### 4052 - When a parent declines recommended treatment, I always repeat their decision back out loud and ask a curious, open-ended question to better understand their rationale.

[ALCAN] This move is flagged conditionally applicable in the framework (it fires when a decline happens). From the Why It Matters block: repeating the decision back, neutrally and without editorializing, shows the parent they have been heard rather than judged. Whatever the outcome, the recommendation, the discussion, and the informed refusal are documented before the family leaves.

Practical guidance, from the What Good Looks Like block:

- [ALCAN] Repeat the parent's decision back in neutral language before responding to it; ask one curious open-ended question, then listen to the whole answer.
- [ALCAN] Address the actual concern that surfaces rather than re-arguing the plan; explain plainly what monitoring can and cannot catch; document the recommendation, discussion, and informed refusal before the family leaves.

## Scripts and examples

**Opening with curiosity (move 4033):** [ALCAN]
> "Can you tell me more about what's making this feel hard right now?"
> "I want to understand your concerns so we can talk through them together."
> "My recommendation is based on what we're seeing clinically and what we know about how cavities tend to progress."

**Naming the standard of care, kindly (move 4033):** [ALCAN]
> "I want to be very transparent with you. Our team practices proactive pediatric dental care, and it becomes difficult for us to continue monitoring disease without appropriate diagnostics or treatment."
> "We completely respect you making decisions that feel right for your family. At the same time, continuing care without X-rays or treatment would place us outside the standard of care we're committed to providing."
> "If our approach doesn't feel like the right fit for your family, we're happy to provide records and help with a smooth transition to another provider."

**Escalating internally (move 4034):** [ALCAN]
> "I'm noticing something that's making it difficult for me to practice to our standard of care, and I want to flag it so we can address it together."
> "I want to make sure we're aligned on this process, because right now it's creating challenges for patient care."

**Responding to a decline (move 4052):** [ALCAN]
> "I'm hearing you say that you're not inclined to move forward with this plan. Can I ask you to tell me a little more about that?"
> "That makes sense, and I appreciate you sharing it. Here's what I want to make sure you know about what we can and cannot catch by watching it."
> "We completely respect you making decisions that feel right for your family. I'll document what we discussed today so we both have a clear record."

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

**Good looks like:** the sourced bullets under each move above. The through-line: curiosity first, boundaries second, documentation always.

**Common pitfalls** [ALCAN where drawn from the Gut Check blocks, otherwise GENERAL]:

- [ALCAN] Responding to hesitation with defensiveness instead of curiosity, or not giving the parent space to fully explain (4033 Gut Check).
- [ALCAN] "Ask and then re-pitch": asking the open question but not actually listening to the answer (4052 Gut Check).
- [ALCAN] Quietly absorbing a recurring team or system problem until a workaround becomes the new standard (4034 Gut Check).
- [ALCAN] Letting a family leave without the informed refusal documented (4052 Gut Check).
- [GENERAL] Jumping straight to the "transition to another provider" language on a first hesitation; the framework sequences it as a later step, after listening and after more than one conversation about the recommendation (the 4033 Gut Check asks "Have we already had more than one conversation about this recommendation?").
