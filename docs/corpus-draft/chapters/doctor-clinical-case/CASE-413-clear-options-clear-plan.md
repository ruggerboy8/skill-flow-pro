# CASE-413: Clear Options to Clear Plan

**Role:** Doctor | **Domain:** Case Acceptance | **Tagline [ALCAN]:** "Guide families to confident decisions"

## Overview

This competency is about closing the loop: options can be discussed verbally, but the room is not done until one clear, mutually agreed plan exists and everyone (parent, RDA, chart) holds the same version of it. The five moves: land one charted plan before leaving the room (4039), introduce the AI technology and proactive philosophy during exams so recommendations have context (4040), ask the parent to say the plan back (4050), give explicit permission to push back (4051), and when cost is the barrier, name the clinically sound alternatives and personally hand the family to the treatment coordinator (4054).

It applies to every doctor, in every treatment planning conversation.

## Why it matters

[ALCAN] From the framework's Why It Matters blocks:

- Treatment conversations that end without a clear decision lead to missed appointments, delayed care, confusion for the team, and frustration for families. "I'll think about it" often means uncertain, overwhelmed, or unclear. Closing the loop means the parent understands the next visit, the RDA knows exactly how to schedule, and the team can prepare. (Move 4039)
- Parents cannot see what the doctor sees on a radiograph, so trust in the diagnosis is really trust in the process. A brief mention that AI reviews every X-ray alongside the doctor makes the process visible, and establishing the proactive philosophy before the first finding means an early-treatment recommendation lands as "this is how we practice" rather than as an upsell. (Move 4040)
- Parents routinely leave with a partial or different understanding than the doctor believes was communicated. The gap surfaces later as confusion, missed appointments, or quietly declined care, and it is cheap to fix only while everyone is still in the room. (Move 4050)
- Many parents will not voice doubts to a doctor unless invited. They nod in the room and then vote with the schedule. Explicit permission moves the objection somewhere it can be addressed, and parents who feel ownership of a decision follow through on it. (Move 4051)
- How the doctor handles the cost moment decides whether the family leaves with a path or a dead end. Acknowledgment preserves dignity, honest alternatives restore agency without compromising care, and the personal handoff matters as much as the alternatives: "the front desk can help you" reads as a dismissal; a warm introduction by name is a plan. (Move 4054)

## The moves

### 4039: One clear plan before leaving the room

**Statement [ALCAN]:** "I always ensure a clear (mutually agreed upon) treatment plan is in place before leaving the room/conversation with parent and RDA."

[ALCAN] Guidance from the framework: discuss options verbally, but select one plan before leaving, and direct the RDA to chart that single plan rather than multiple unresolved possibilities. Communicate timing and expectations for the next visit, and make sure the parent and the RDA heard the same thing.

### 4040: Introduce the AI technology and proactive philosophy

**Statement [ALCAN]:** "I always briefly introduce our AI technology and explain our proactive philosophy of care during exam appointments."

[ALCAN] Guidance from the framework: work it naturally into the exam, 15 to 30 seconds, never a lecture. Mention it before findings are presented so the framing is already in place, connect the technology to the philosophy of proactive care and early detection, and show the finding on screen when it helps the parent see what you see. (The imaging platform is Overjet; the DFI and RDA frameworks preview it by name in their credibility moves, actions 93 and 70.)

### 4050: Ask the parent to share the plan back

**Statement [ALCAN]:** "After presenting a treatment plan, I always ask the parent to share their understanding of the plan back to me, so I can fill any gaps before they leave." *(conditionally applicable)*

[ALCAN] Guidance from the framework: frame the request as a check on your own explaining, never as a quiz of the parent. Listen to the whole answer without interrupting, fill gaps warmly, confirm the corrected understanding, and make sure the RDA hears the final version so the charted plan matches the spoken one.

### 4051: Give explicit permission to push back

**Statement [ALCAN]:** "When presenting a treatment plan, I always give parents explicit permission to push back and invite them to explore alternatives with me." *(conditionally applicable)*

[ALCAN] Guidance from the framework: offer the invitation out loud after the recommendation, not only when hesitation appears; watch for nonverbal hesitation and extend it again; receive pushback with curiosity rather than defensiveness; and talk through alternatives honestly, including where clinical needs limit the options.

### 4054: Meet the cost barrier with alternatives and a personal handoff

**Statement [ALCAN]:** "When a parent communicates that cost is a barrier to care, I always acknowledge the concern without judgment, name the clinically sound alternatives that could change the cost, and personally connect the family with our treatment coordinator." *(conditionally applicable)*

[ALCAN] Guidance from the framework: acknowledge warmly the moment cost is raised, name which clinically sound alternatives change the cost picture and be honest about which do not, never present an option that compromises care as a cost fix, walk the family to the treatment coordinator (or bring the coordinator in) by name, and confirm the family leaves with a concrete next step, not just sympathy. This is the follow-through to the budget discovery in CASE-411 (move 4053).

## Scripts and examples

All quoted from the framework's doctor script resources [ALCAN]:

**Closing to one plan (move 4039):**
> "Based on what we've discussed and what matters most to you, I'd recommend treating this cavity at the next visit with a white crown using oral sedation so your child stays comfortable and the tooth looks natural."

> "Of the options we talked about, which one is feeling like the best fit for your family so my team can get the right plan in place for you?"

> "So the plan will be to treat this cavity at the next visit using oral sedation."

**Introducing the technology (move 4040):**
> "One thing we do here that I really value: every X-ray gets reviewed by AI technology alongside my own eyes. It's like a second opinion built into every visit, and it helps us catch things early, which is exactly how we like to practice: proactive rather than wait-and-see."

> "Let me show you what I'm seeing here, so you can see it too."

**Teach-back (move 4050):**
> "I know that was a lot of information. Before we wrap up, I just want to make sure we're on the same page. What's your understanding of the plan from here?"

> "That's exactly right, and thank you for walking me through it. One thing I want to add so it's completely clear..."

> "That's my fault for not explaining it well. Let me try that part again."

**Permission to push back (move 4051):**
> "You know your child and your family best. If this recommendation doesn't feel like the right direction for you, let me know so we can talk through alternatives together."

> "I'd rather hear a concern now than have you leave unsure about it."

> "That's a fair question, and I'm glad you asked it. Let me explain why I landed where I did."

**The cost moment (move 4054):**
> "I completely understand, and I'm glad you told me. There are a couple of clinically sound ways we could approach this that change the cost picture. Let me walk you through them."

> "I want to be straight with you: this part is not somewhere I'd recommend economizing, because it affects how long the treatment lasts. But here is where we do have real flexibility."

> "I'd also like to introduce you to Sarah, our treatment coordinator. She can put real numbers to each option and talk through what works for your family."

(The name "Sarah" in that script is illustrative. Use your location's actual treatment coordinator by name.)

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

[ALCAN] Sourced from the What Good Looks Like and gut check resources:

- The parent could explain the next visit to someone else, the RDA will schedule exactly what the doctor intends, and one plan (not several possibilities) is charted.
- The AI and philosophy mention is brief, natural, and lands before findings, so a parent could explain how the practice approaches early detection.
- Teach-back happens after every treatment plan conversation, gaps are treated as the doctor's explaining to improve, and the RDA hears the same final plan the parent did.
- The pushback invitation is said out loud, hesitation gets explored, and pushback is met with curiosity.
- Cost conversations end with a workable path and a named handoff, never a wave toward the front desk.

[ALCAN] Pitfalls implied by the gut checks:

- Ending the conversation without closing the loop, or settling for "any questions?" instead of asking for the parent's understanding.
- Letting the AI mention become a gadget detour or a lecture.
- Treating a comprehension gap as the parent's failure.
- Assuming the parent felt free to disagree without saying the invitation out loud.
- Naming a "cheaper alternative" that quietly compromises care.
