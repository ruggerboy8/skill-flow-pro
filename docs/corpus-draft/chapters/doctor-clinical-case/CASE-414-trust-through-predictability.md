# CASE-414: Trust Through Predictability

**Role:** Doctor | **Domain:** Case Acceptance | **Tagline [ALCAN]:** "Clear expectations prevent surprises"

## Overview

This competency is about making the future feel planned. Three moves: state the next appointment plainly while parent and RDA are both in the room (4041), honor the treatment and sedation plan a previous provider set whenever it remains clinically appropriate (4042), and introduce age-appropriate future recommendations early so milestones never feel sudden or sales-driven (4043).

It applies to every doctor, and it matters most in a multi-provider practice where families may see a different doctor at the next visit.

## Why it matters

[ALCAN] From the framework's Why It Matters blocks:

- Most broken treatment plans do not break in the operatory. They break between rooms: the parent hears one thing, the RDA schedules another, and weeks later a confused family misses an appointment nobody quite agreed on. Stating the next appointment plainly, with both the parent and RDA present, makes the handoff predictable and the plan survives the walk to the front desk. (Move 4041)
- Families expect consistency between doctors, and unexpected changes erode trust. Respecting previously established plans reinforces trust between providers, builds confidence for families, and ensures continuity of care. (Move 4042)
- When families understand what is coming next, dental care feels intentional rather than reactive. Anticipatory guidance normalizes radiographs, sealants, fluoride treatments, and orthodontic evaluations before they arrive, and makes later recommendations less likely to be perceived as inconsistent, unnecessary, or financially motivated. (Move 4043)

## The moves

### 4041: Make the next appointment unmistakable

**Statement [ALCAN]:** "I always ensure the parent and RDA clearly understand the next appointment before leaving the room."

[ALCAN] Guidance from the framework: state the next appointment's purpose, treatment, sedation plan, and rough timing out loud, while both the parent and the RDA are present and listening. Confirm the RDA knows exactly what to schedule, and invite the parent's questions about the next visit specifically. The gut check test: could this parent explain the next appointment to someone else tonight, and will the RDA schedule it without guessing?

### 4042: Honor the existing treatment and sedation plan

**Statement [ALCAN]:** "I always honor the existing treatment and sedation plan whenever possible"

[ALCAN] Guidance from the framework: when seeing another provider's patient, carefully review the existing treatment and sedation plan before initiating care, and honor it whenever it remains clinically appropriate. Before modifying, pause to weigh the trust impact on the family and the alignment between providers, and change only when truly clinically necessary, never on personal preference alone. When updated information may warrant a change (for example, months have passed and caries progress quickly in primary teeth), verify first with an updated image, then explain the reasoning transparently.

### 4043: Introduce future recommendations early

**Statement [ALCAN]:** "I always introduce age-appropriate future recommendations early so families understand what to expect as their child grows."

[ALCAN] Guidance from the framework: plant expectations early and revisit them at future visits, with calm, educational explanations that are not sales-oriented, reinforced with statements like "we'll revisit this when the time comes." This pairs with the referral checkpoints in CLIN-404 (ortho around 7-8 and 12-13, oral surgery for third molars around 15-16, move 4016).

## Scripts and examples

All quoted from the framework's doctor script resources [ALCAN]:

**Closing the room on the next visit (move 4041):**
> "So the next visit will be the two crowns on the lower right, with oral sedation, ideally within the next few weeks. Jenni will get that scheduled with you before you head out."

> "Anything about that next appointment that feels unclear or concerning?"

**Honoring or revising a prior plan (move 4042):**
> "I see that Dr. Jones recommended a filling for this tooth. I reviewed the chart and agree with that plan, so we'll go ahead with that today."

> "I see the plan was to complete treatment with oral sedation today. I agree that your child is a safe candidate for that, so we'll move forward with that plan."

> "It's been a few months since the last visit, and cavities in baby teeth can progress quickly. I'd like to take an updated X-ray first to make sure the original plan is still the best one."

> "Based on what I'm seeing today, I do think we need to adjust the plan slightly from what was originally discussed. I want to walk you through why so it makes sense."

**Anticipatory guidance by age (move 4043):**
> Infant / early toddler: "At this age, we're really focused on prevention. As your child gets older, we'll typically recommend fluoride varnish every six months to help protect the teeth."

> Around age 3: "Right now we're just visually monitoring the teeth. Around age four, we usually start taking cavity-detecting X-rays so we can check between the back teeth, because sometimes cavities can hide in those areas where we can't see them."

> Around age 4-5: "In the next year or so, we'll likely start talking about sealants to help protect the grooves of the back teeth."

> General framing: "I like to share this early so nothing ever feels surprising. We'll always talk through it again when the time comes."

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

[ALCAN] Sourced from the What Good Looks Like and gut check resources:

- The next appointment's purpose, treatment, sedation, and timing are said out loud in the room, and the loop closes there rather than being left for the front desk to reconstruct.
- Existing plans are reviewed before care begins, honored when clinically appropriate, and changed only with clear clinical reasoning explained transparently, so families experience consistency between providers.
- Future milestones are introduced as routine, in an educational rather than sales-oriented tone, and today's conversation supports whichever doctor sees the child next year.

[ALCAN] Pitfalls implied by the gut checks:

- Leaving timing unsaid, or leaving the RDA to guess what to schedule.
- Changing another doctor's plan out of personal preference rather than clinical necessity.
- Springing a milestone recommendation on a family with no prior seeding, so it feels sudden or financially motivated.

[GENERAL] A universal pitfall in multi-provider groups: criticizing or second-guessing a colleague's plan in front of the family. Even a justified change should be framed as new information, the way the move 4042 scripts model it, not as a correction of the other doctor.
