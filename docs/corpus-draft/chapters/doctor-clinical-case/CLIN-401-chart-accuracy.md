# CLIN-401: Chart Accuracy

**Role:** Doctor | **Domain:** Clinical | **Tagline [ALCAN]:** "Accurate records protect patients and teams"

## Overview

This competency covers how doctors keep the patient chart complete, current, and trustworthy. The through-line across all three moves is the same habit: the doctor verbalizes findings out loud, in a consistent sequence, so the RDA and the AI documentation system can capture them in real time. That applies to the full exam narrative, the baseline record at new patient exams, and the odontogram at every encounter.

It applies to every doctor, at every patient encounter.

## Why it matters

[ALCAN] From the framework's Why It Matters blocks:

- Consistency ensures nothing is missed, improves documentation accuracy, and allows AI note-taking tools to function reliably. Comprehensive charting allows for comprehensive treatment planning: if the airway evaluation or BMI is missed, a high-risk patient could unintentionally be planned for oral sedation, or not be offered the most comprehensive and safe options available. (Move 189)
- An accurate baseline record is essential for clinical accuracy, continuity of care, and risk management. Documenting existing restorations, missing teeth, areas of concern, and prior treatment at the first visit prevents confusion later about what was already present versus what developed under our care. A clearly established baseline protects the practice from liability. (Move 4001)
- The odontogram is a living clinical document. In pediatric dentistry especially, teeth are constantly erupting, exfoliating, and being restored, so the chart can quickly become outdated if it is not actively maintained. An inaccurate odontogram creates confusion for providers and assistants, complicates treatment planning, and increases the risk of documentation errors, particularly when patients are seen by different providers within the practice. (Move 4002)

## The moves

### 189: Verbalize the exam findings in their entirety

**Statement [ALCAN]:** "I always verbalize the exam findings in it's entirety to the RDA/AI- including: extraoral findings, TMJ, intraoral soft tissue findings, current dentition, hard tissue findings, radiographic findings, occlusion, and airway."

[ALCAN] Guidance from the framework: use a consistent verbal exam sequence so that every critical part of the evaluation is completed and documented, reducing the risk that important findings are missed. Speaking findings clearly and in the same order each time allows the RDA and AI documentation tools to accurately capture the exam, improving charting quality and reducing the need for corrections after the visit. The What Good Looks Like resource adds one prerequisite: ensure the patient was formally presented to you by the RDA so medical history, BMI, and chief complaint are captured before the exam narrative begins.

### 4001: Dictate a clear baseline at new patient exams

**Statement [ALCAN]:** "I always verbally dictate to RDA/AI a clear and accurate baseline oral health status at new patient exams- current teeth present, existing treatment, and any findings present."

[ALCAN] Guidance from the framework: at new patient exams, verbally call out existing restorations, missing teeth, and prior treatment so the RDA and AI can accurately document the patient's starting dentition. The What Good Looks Like resource is specific about ordering: fully chart the baseline record **before** entering new caries or treatment recommendations, so future providers can easily distinguish existing conditions from new findings.

### 4002: Update the odontogram at every encounter

**Statement [ALCAN]:** "I always update the odontogram and any clinical findings by verbally dictating them to the RDA/AI at each patient encounter."

[ALCAN] Guidance from the framework: at every exam, verbally call out erupted, exfoliated, restored, or missing teeth so the RDA and AI can update the odontogram in real time. Review the existing odontogram before beginning the exam, confirm it matches what you see clinically and radiographically, and confirm the RDA and AI system captured the updates accurately before completing the exam documentation.

## Scripts and examples

All quoted from the framework's doctor script resources [ALCAN]:

**Full exam narration (move 189):**
> "Soft tissue findings: fistula noted for tooth #B. Hard tissue findings: caries on 3-occlusal, hypo on 8-facial. Airway: mallampati 2, brodsky 3."

The script resource models the flow as extraoral first ("no abnormal findings observed"), then intraoral soft tissue, then hard tissue findings, and so on through the full sequence in the statement.

**Baseline dictation (move 4001):**
> "Calling out existing stainless steel crown on A, existing occlusal composite on J, missing tooth S."

> "Existing composite restoration on K, sealant present on 19, stainless steel crown on T."

> "Parent reports crown placed by previous dentist on tooth B."

**Odontogram updates (move 4002):**
> "Let's update the odontogram. Tooth T has exfoliated since the last visit."

> "#30 has erupted since the last exam, let's add it to the chart and plan a sealant."

> "Existing stainless steel crown on K. Confirming it's already charted."

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

[ALCAN] Sourced from the What Good Looks Like resources:

- Consistently use the same exam flow for every patient, and speak all findings aloud clearly and completely enough for both team members and AI tools to capture them.
- At new patient exams, the chart clearly reflects a complete and accurate starting point for care before any new diagnosis is entered.
- The odontogram is reviewed and updated at every exam appointment, so any provider reviewing the chart can quickly and confidently understand the patient's dental status.

[ALCAN] Pitfalls implied by the gut check resources:

- Skipping a section of the exam narrative or varying the sequence, which degrades both RDA capture and AI capture.
- Diagnosing new disease before the baseline charting is complete.
- Ending the exam without confirming the RDA and AI actually captured the updates.

[GENERAL] A universal pitfall in verbal-dictation workflows: mumbling or trailing off mid-finding. If the person or system transcribing cannot hear it, it was never charted.
