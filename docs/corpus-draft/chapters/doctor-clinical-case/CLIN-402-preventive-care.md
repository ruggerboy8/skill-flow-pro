# CLIN-402: Preventive Care

**Role:** Doctor | **Domain:** Clinical | **Tagline [ALCAN]:** "Early detection enables proactive intervention"

## Overview

This competency is about catching disease early and treating it honestly. Two of the moves are about the record and the offer: chart incipient lesions as real decay, and offer minimally invasive treatment (SDF, Curodont) or sealants regardless of insurance coverage. The other is about language: explain cavities to parents by tooth layer, never with minimizing words like "spot" or "watch."

It applies to every doctor, at every exam where early lesions or sealant candidates are found.

## Why it matters

[ALCAN] From the framework's Why It Matters blocks:

- Early carious lesions guide preventive care, monitoring, and parent education. If they are not documented, opportunities for early intervention are lost. Charting enamel lesions as decay ensures they are tracked over time and prevents language that unintentionally minimizes the presence of disease. Combining clinical expertise with AI-assisted detection allows earlier intervention and more transparent conversations about cavity risk. (Move 4003)
- Minimizing language like "just a spot" or "a tiny cavity we're watching" can lead to confusion, treatment delays, and downplay the seriousness of a condition. Explaining decay by tooth layer offers a straightforward, visual framework that supports informed decision-making and fosters trust. (Move 4004)
- Sealants are a highly effective preventive measure against cavities, particularly in the deep grooves of molars and premolars where decay often begins. Offering them regardless of insurance coverage ensures families are fully informed of all preventive options. (Move 4005)
- When lesions are confined to enamel, minimally invasive treatments like SDF or Curodont can help arrest or remineralize, preserving tooth structure and potentially preventing restorative treatment. Treatment decisions should prioritize clinical benefit and disease stage over insurance limitations. (Move 4006)

## The moves

### 4003: Chart incipient lesions

**Statement [ALCAN]:** "I always verbalize to the RDA/AI to chart any incipient lesions so that preventative care can be offered or teeth can be appropriately monitored radiographically."

[ALCAN] Guidance from the framework: during exams, verbally identify incipient enamel lesions so the RDA and AI can document them. Review the Overjet AI overlay during radiographic evaluation, clearly label incipient lesions as caries for monitoring, and avoid minimizing language such as "watch" that could obscure the presence of disease. Incorporate the finding into the patient's preventive or treatment plan when appropriate.

### 4004: Explain cavities by tooth layer

**Statement [ALCAN]:** "I always explain cavities clearly- indicating to the parent there is a cavity in the first, second or third layer of tooth- instead of saying there is a 'spot' 'watch' or other minimizing language."

[ALCAN] Guidance from the framework: describe disease accurately and confidently without causing alarm, use layer-based explanations to help parents understand disease progression, and connect the recommended treatment to the depth of the cavity. Frame preventive care as proactive early treatment, not as dismissal of disease.

### 4005: Offer sealants regardless of coverage

**Statement [ALCAN]:** "I always offer sealants on healthy posterior primary and permanent teeth, regardless of insurance coverage."

[ALCAN] Guidance from the framework: evaluate posterior teeth for sealant candidacy at every exam and offer sealants as a routine preventive measure, not only when insurance covers them. Select materials thoughtfully: the resources call out Equia Forte (a glass ionomer based option) for cases where isolation or behavior makes traditional sealants difficult, including cooperative younger children around ages 4 to 5, as a simple preventive procedure that does not require Isodry isolation.

### 4006: Offer SDF or Curodont for incipient lesions

**Statement [ALCAN]:** "I always offer SDF (primary teeth) or Curodont (Permanent teeth) for incipient lesions."

[ALCAN] Guidance from the framework: identify early enamel lesions using both clinical judgment and AI-assisted radiographic analysis. When lesions are confined to enamel, offer the minimally invasive option matched to the dentition: SDF for primary teeth, Curodont for permanent teeth. When lesions extend past the DEJ into dentin, transition to definitive restorative planning (see move 4012 in CLIN-404). Base recommendations on disease stage and clinical benefit, not insurance coverage.

## Scripts and examples

All quoted from the framework's doctor script resources [ALCAN]:

**Charting dictation (move 4003):**
> "Incipient lesion on B mesial. Please mark decay and treatment plan SDF."

> "Overjet is highlighting early enamel breakdown on I distal. Let's chart that as decay so we can monitor it closely."

**Layer-based cavity explanations (move 4004):**
> "There is a cavity in the first layer of the tooth. The good news is that at this stage we often have preventive options, and I'd love to talk with you about treatments like SDF or Curodont."

> "This cavity has reached the second layer of the tooth. Once decay reaches this layer, it tends to spread more quickly, so we typically recommend treating it with a filling or crown."

> "This cavity has progressed into the third layer of the tooth where the nerve lives, which means we may need to discuss more involved treatment to protect the tooth and prevent infection."

**Sealant offers (move 4005):**
> "One of the best ways we can help prevent cavities is with sealants. They act like a protective coating over the grooves of the teeth where cavities often start."

> "For some reason, insurance plans don't always include premolars in their sealant benefits, but I still like to offer them because they can provide an extra layer of protection if that's something you'd like to do."

> "For younger kids or teeth that are harder to keep perfectly dry, we sometimes use a glass ionomer based sealant material that still provides protection and is easier to place."

**Minimally invasive options (move 4006):**
> "Because we use advanced AI-assisted imaging, we're able to detect very early cavity lesions. That's actually a good thing. It allows us to treat them preventively before they progress."

> "When cavities are caught at the enamel stage, we sometimes have options that don't involve drilling. Treatments like Silver Diamine Fluoride or Curodont can help stop or remineralize early areas of decay."

> "Even when insurance doesn't cover something, I still want families to know about all the options so they can decide if they'd like to take a proactive approach."

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

[ALCAN] Sourced from the What Good Looks Like and gut check resources:

- Overjet AI overlays are reviewed consistently during radiographic evaluation, and early lesions are verbally called out and charted as caries.
- Cavity depth is explained by layer, the recommended treatment is connected to that depth, and the parent's understanding is confirmed.
- Every posterior tooth is evaluated for sealant candidacy at every exam, with recommendations based on clinical benefit rather than insurance limitations.
- Parents clearly understand that early detection is what unlocks minimally invasive options.

[ALCAN] Pitfalls implied by the gut checks:

- Saying "watch" or "spot" and thereby leaving disease off the chart and out of the conversation.
- Offering sealants only when insurance covers them.
- Offering SDF or Curodont for lesions that have already passed the DEJ, where definitive treatment is the standard (see CLIN-404).

[GENERAL] A pitfall in any early-intervention conversation: presenting the preventive option so casually that the parent hears "optional and unimportant." The layer framing exists precisely so the offer carries clinical weight.

**Q: How does a charted incipient lesion change the recall and imaging plan?**
A: [ALCAN] Incipient lesions place the patient in the high caries risk pathway: bitewings and any necessary PAs every 6 months (see move 4008 in CLIN-403), and the lesion is monitored radiographically over time.

**Q: When is a lesion no longer a candidate for preventive treatment?**
A: [ALCAN] Once decay extends past the DEJ into dentin, minimally invasive approaches are no longer predictable on their own, and definitive restorative treatment (composite, crown, extraction) is the standard of care (move 4012, CLIN-404).
