# CLIN-403: Smart Imaging and Diagnostics

**Role:** Doctor | **Domain:** Clinical | **Tagline [ALCAN]:** "Right images at the right time"

## Overview

This competency covers the doctor's imaging discipline: chart a caries risk assessment for every patient, and let that risk drive what images are taken and when. The five moves form a system: risk assessment (4007) sets the interval, high-risk patients get bitewings and PAs every 6 months (4008), teeth headed for extraction or pulp therapy get a PA first (4009), new patient and referral exams get a comprehensive evaluation with a full series (4010), and panos are recommended around age 6 to 7 and every three years after (4011), regardless of insurance coverage.

It applies to every doctor, for every patient.

## Why it matters

[ALCAN] From the framework's Why It Matters blocks:

- Caries risk is the hinge of preventive dentistry. It determines radiograph frequency, preventive strategy, and recall interval. When risk is not charted, those decisions still get made, but without a documented basis. A charted assessment makes the reasoning visible to the doctor at the next visit, to any colleague who sees the child, and to the parent. (Move 4007)
- Patients with elevated caries risk are significantly more likely to develop rapidly progressing interproximal lesions that are not visible in a clinical exam alone. Many cavities are radiographically detectable before they are clinically visible, so timely imaging is essential to avoid missed or delayed diagnoses. The framework also notes that, by definition, children on government-funded healthcare programs and low SES (Medicaid) are in high caries risk status, so following AAPD guidelines on radiographs every 6 months is appropriate for primary and transitional dentition. (Move 4008)
- Periapical radiographs offer diagnostic insight beyond bitewings when considering pulp therapy or extraction: extent of caries relative to the pulp, root morphology, and surrounding bone. This supports accurate diagnosis, informed consent, and risk management. (Move 4009)
- Limited exams for new patients often underrepresent disease. Evaluating the entire oral environment, with high-quality in-house imaging, lets doctors determine the right treatment plan and sedation pathway before committing to either. (Move 4010)
- A panoramic radiograph offers a comprehensive view of developing dentition, jaws, and surrounding anatomy, enabling early detection of ectopic canines, unerupted teeth, cysts, and developmental anomalies. Failing to review panoramic images consistently increases the risk of missed diagnoses. (Move 4011)

## The moves

### 4007: Chart a caries risk assessment for every patient

**Statement [ALCAN]:** "I always chart an appropriate caries risk assessment for every patient."

[ALCAN] Guidance from the framework: chart risk for every patient, new and recall alike. Update the assessment when findings change rather than letting an old status ride. Let the charted risk visibly drive radiograph interval, preventive plan, and recall timing, and chart it so a colleague could find the child's risk status and understand its basis at a glance.

### 4008: Bitewings and PAs every 6 months for high-risk patients

**Statement [ALCAN]:** "I always ensure bitewings and any necessary PA's are taken every 6 months for patients with incipient lesions, history of caries or treatment in last 2 years, history of dental trauma, pulp therapy or other indications that would make them high caries risk or require more frequent follow up."

[ALCAN] Guidance from the framework: individualize radiographic intervals based on disease activity and risk rather than applying them uniformly. Correctly chart incipient lesions and areas of concern so the RDAs know the patient is high caries risk. Once a patient has been stable and cavity-free for at least two years, imaging can often be spaced out again.

### 4009: PA before extraction, pulp therapy, or deep caries treatment

**Statement [ALCAN]:** "I always instruct RDAs to take PAs for any teeth planned for extraction or pulp therapy, or with caries approaching the pulp."

[ALCAN] Guidance from the framework: obtain the diagnostic PA before confirming the treatment plan, evaluate pulpal status, root morphology, resorption, and surrounding bone, and confirm the image quality and diagnostic value before finalizing the plan.

### 4010: Comprehensive evaluation and full series for new patient, limited, or referral exams

**Statement [ALCAN]:** "I always perform a comprehensive evaluation and full series of high-quality radiographs for new patient limited, or referral exams."

[ALCAN] Guidance from the framework: even when a patient presents with a specific concern or referral, evaluate the whole oral environment. This matters most before sedation: the framework stresses not discovering additional cavities after sedation has been scheduled or started. Obtain high-quality in-house images rather than relying solely on outside radiographs, and present the complete diagnostic picture before discussing treatment priorities. The gut check calls out one temptation directly: do not avoid recommended radiographs to appease a parent or save them money in the moment if doing so leads to incomplete information.

### 4011: Pano at new patient exams (around age 6-7+) and every three years

**Statement [ALCAN]:** "I always recommend a pano at new patient exams (around age 6-7+) and every three years thereafter, regardless of insurance coverage."

[ALCAN] Guidance from the framework: review panoramic imaging status during every exam, recommend proactively when a pano is overdue or unavailable, use it to evaluate eruption patterns, impacted canines, pathology, and developmental anomalies, and document both the recommendation and the parent's decision.

All quoted from the framework's doctor script resources [ALCAN]:

**Communicating risk (move 4007):**
> "Based on what we're seeing today, your child is at higher risk for cavities right now. That means we'll want to check with X-rays a little more often and be more proactive with things like fluoride, so we catch anything early."

> "Good news: everything we're seeing puts your child at low risk, so we can be more conservative with X-rays going forward."

**High-risk imaging cadence (move 4008):**
> "For patients with higher cavity risk, we typically take bitewing X-rays about every six months so we can monitor closely and make sure we're catching anything early."

> "Once your child has been cavity-free and stable for a couple of years, we can often space those X-rays out again."

To the team: "High caries risk. Let's take updated bitewings today and plan to repeat them every 6 months until they've been stable and cavity-free for at least two years."

**Ordering a PA (move 4009):**
To the team: "Please take a PA of tooth K so we can evaluate the pulp and roots before deciding between pulp therapy or extraction."

To the parent: "To make sure we choose the right treatment, we're going to take a more detailed X-ray of this tooth. That allows us to evaluate the nerve and surrounding bone before making a final recommendation."

**Comprehensive evaluation (move 4010):**
> "Mrs. Smith, to provide the most accurate diagnosis and a complete understanding of your child's oral health, we'll need to take some updated radiographs today. While we're happy to review previous X-rays, our imaging technology allows us to evaluate everything at a very high level so we can be confident we aren't missing anything."

> "When we're planning treatment for children who may need sedation, it's important that we understand the full picture ahead of time. We don't want to discover additional cavities after sedation has already been scheduled or started."

> "We can absolutely start by focusing on the main concern today. At the same time, I want you to have the full picture so we can work together to prioritize what should be addressed first."

**Recommending the pano (move 4011):**
> "It's been about three years since your child's last panoramic X-ray, so we recommend taking another one today. This allows us to monitor how the permanent teeth are developing, complete a cancer screening, and make sure everything is erupting properly."

> "While insurance may not always include Panoramic benefits, we still recommend them because they allow us to detect potential issues early, check for growth and development, extra or missing teeth and perform a cancer screening."

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

[ALCAN] Sourced from the What Good Looks Like and gut check resources:

- Every patient seen today has a charted risk assessment that matches what was actually seen in the chair, and imaging decisions visibly follow from it.
- High-risk patients have bitewings within the last 6 months; PAs are added when clinical findings warrant.
- No pulp therapy or extraction plan is confirmed without a diagnostic PA.
- New patient charts contain a comprehensive exam and a complete set of current, high-quality radiographs, so families leave with the full picture and no surprise findings later.
- Pano recommendations and the parent's accept or decline decision are documented in the chart.

[ALCAN] Pitfalls implied by the gut checks:

- Applying one radiographic interval to everyone instead of individualizing by risk.
- Treating a limited or referral exam as license to look at only one tooth.
- Skipping recommended radiographs to appease a parent or save money in the moment.
- Letting a pano quietly lapse past three years because no one checked its status.
