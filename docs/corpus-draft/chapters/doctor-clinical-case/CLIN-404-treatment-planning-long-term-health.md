# CLIN-404: Treatment Planning for Long Term Health

**Role:** Doctor | **Domain:** Clinical | **Tagline [ALCAN]:** "Evidence-based care for lasting outcomes"

## Overview

This is the largest doctor competency in the Clinical domain: twelve moves that together define how Alcan doctors treatment plan. Three threads run through it:

1. **Definitive over provisional.** Treat dentin lesions definitively (4012), recommend crowns for structurally high-risk lesions (198), plan anterior GA cases with heightened caution (199), include the IPC in the plan when the pulp is approximated (204), evaluate space maintenance at every primary molar extraction (4014), and plan definitively for families with real barriers to returning (203).
2. **Fewer visits.** Consolidate treatment (192), offer same-day sealants, hall crowns, and space maintainers (193), and ask mid-appointment permission to keep going when a child is doing well (4044).
3. **The long arc.** Offer both white and silver crown options (4013), evaluate ortho and oral surgery referrals at defined ages (4016), and never exceed AAPD oral sedation dosing limits (205).

It applies to every doctor, whenever treatment is being planned or delivered.

## Why it matters

[ALCAN] Selected from the framework's Why It Matters blocks, by thread:

- **Definitive over provisional.** Caries that has progressed into dentin is active disease unlikely to arrest without definitive treatment (4012). Studies consistently show primary teeth restored with full-coverage crowns have significantly higher success rates than multi-surface fillings, whose failure rates can approach 40 to 50 percent within two years; cumulative failure across multiple restorations means repeat procedures, repeat sedation, and higher cost (198). Anterior teeth carry extra emotional weight for parents, so realistic options (zirconia crowns or extraction) must be discussed before GA, not discovered during it (199). IPCs preserve vitality and belong in the plan, visible to the financial team, not added by surprise on treatment day (204). Premature primary tooth loss can cost arch length; space maintenance protects the developing dentition (4014). For remote and charitable-care families there is no one to "watch" cavities, so we plan with a public health mindset and definitive, longest lasting options (203).
- **Fewer visits.** Consolidating care reduces the financial, emotional, and logistical burden of repeat visits for families and improves chair utilization for the practice (192, 193). A good visit is a scarce resource; asking the parent's permission mid-appointment honors their role while saving the family a trip and a sedation event (4044).
- **The long arc.** Families value choice and transparency; equitable care means every family is informed of both SSC and zirconia options (4013). Early ortho evaluation and third molar monitoring prevent more complex issues later (4016). AAPD MRD adherence is a patient safety and liability floor that is never crossed (205).

## The moves

### 192: Consolidate treatment into fewer visits

**Statement [ALCAN]:** "I always look for opportunities to consolidate treatment into fewer visits, so families save trips, time out of school, and repeat sedation events."

[ALCAN] The gut check sets concrete targets: attempt at least two quadrants of primary dentition treatment, or one quadrant of adult dentition treatment, per sedation or nitrous appointment when appropriate, always balanced against the child's behavior, comfort, and tolerance, and against the overall schedule so other patients are not harmed.

### 193: Offer same-day sealants, hall crowns, and space maintainers

**Statement [ALCAN]:** "I always offer same day sealants, hall crowns, and space maintainers - and always consider other same day restorative treatment when the schedule permits."

[ALCAN] Guidance from the framework: quick procedures such as sealants, hall crowns, space maintainer impressions, or recements should always be done same day. When the schedule permits, bring over other restorative same-day procedures too (for example a small buccal composite). The What Good Looks Like resource prioritizes: sealants, SDF, Curodont, spacer impressions or fabrication of unilateral spacers, small restorations, and urgent care for limited exams.

### 198: Recommend crowns for structurally high-risk primary lesions

**Statement [ALCAN]:** "I always recommend a crown as the treatment option with the highest long-term success rate for primary tooth lesions that are structurally high-risk - specifically MO lesions on first primary molars, MOD lesions on any molar, lesions more than 50% into dentin, anterior lesions involving 3 or more surfaces or the incisal edge, and lesions of any size in a child being treated under general anesthesia."

[ALCAN] Guidance from the framework: recommend the crown confidently and do not present fillings as a comparable option for these patterns; their higher failure rates mean they cannot meet the standard for predictable long-term care.

### 199: Anterior teeth under GA: caution and transparency

**Statement [ALCAN]:** "I treatment plan anterior teeth under general anesthesia with heightened caution and transparency, recommending zirconia crowns for restorable teeth or extraction when prognosis is poor."

[ALCAN] Guidance from the framework: plan zirconia crowns for restorable anterior teeth under anesthesia (no strip crowns in the plan; the treating doctor during GA may perform strip crowns if deemed appropriate). Prepare parents for the worst-case scenarios in advance, financially (zirconia) and emotionally (extraction), because caries progress quickly and the full picture often only emerges under GA. Give families time to apply for CareCredit or financing, or come to terms with extraction, before the day of anesthesia.

### 203: Plan definitively for long-distance and charitable-care families

**Statement [ALCAN]:** "When a family travels a long distance to reach us or comes through a charitable care program, I always treatment plan definitively rather than watch-and-wait, so essential care is completed in as few visits as possible." *(conditionally applicable)*

[ALCAN] Guidance from the framework: evaluate every tooth as if this may be the child's only dental visit. Plan treatment for any active lesion in primary teeth, even early lesions that might otherwise be monitored. Use full coverage restorations (SSC or zirconia) for primary teeth with restorative needs, and design the plan to complete in one or two visits, considering sedation or GA to achieve comprehensive care in a single appointment. The Why It Matters block names St. David's Foundation as an example of the charitable programs this applies to.

### 204: Include the IPC in the plan when the pulp is approximated

**Statement [ALCAN]:** "I always include an indirect pulp cap (IPC) in the treatment plan when a lesion approximates the pulp and pulp protection may be needed."

[ALCAN] Guidance from the framework: the IPC is appropriate when the tooth is asymptomatic and a clear band of dentin remains between caries and pulp. Put it in the treatment plan so the financial team can review it with the family before treatment; do not add it unexpectedly on the day of treatment or write it off without discussion.

### 205: Never exceed AAPD oral sedation dosing limits

**Statement [ALCAN]:** "I always adhere to the AAPD Maximum Recommended Dose (MRD) and weight-based dosing guidelines for all oral sedation medications and never exceed these limits."

[ALCAN] The gut check resource carries the reference numbers: weight-based dosing per AAPD guidelines (Meperidine 1-2 mg/kg, Midazolam 0.25-0.5 mg/kg, Diazepam 0.2-0.3 mg/kg, Hydroxyzine 0.5-1 mg/kg) and MRD ceilings (Meperidine 50 mg, Midazolam 15 mg, Diazepam 10 mg, Hydroxyzine 50 mg). For combination sedation, reduce each medication dose; full MRD dosing of multiple medications simultaneously is never used. The plan should clearly meet AAPD and TSBDE standards of care if reviewed. **These numbers are quoted from the framework resource; verify against the current AAPD reference before relying on them chairside.**

### 4012: Definitive restorative treatment past the DEJ

**Statement [ALCAN]:** "I always offer definitive restorative treatment (composite, crown, EXT) for lesions extending past the DEJ."

[ALCAN] Guidance from the framework: once decay reaches dentin, monitoring alone is no longer appropriate and minimally invasive options are no longer predictable on their own. Determine lesion depth using both clinical judgment and the AI overlay, recommend definitive treatment, and document the recommendation and the family's decision.

### 4013: Offer both white and silver crown options

**Statement [ALCAN]:** "I always offer both white and silver crown options when full-coverage restorations are indicated."

[ALCAN] Guidance from the framework: both stainless steel and zirconia crowns are clinically appropriate full-coverage options with strong long-term success. Present both without pre-deciding for the family, explain the esthetic and benefits-coverage differences, defer detailed insurance conversations to the practice manager, and note that zirconia is more technique-sensitive, which may warrant a higher sedation level to complete comfortably.

### 4014: Evaluate space maintenance at primary molar extractions

**Statement [ALCAN]:** "When a primary molar is extracted or planned for extraction, I always evaluate whether space maintenance is indicated based on the patient's age, dental development, eruption status, and clinical circumstances, and document the decision and family discussion when applicable." *(conditionally applicable)*

[ALCAN] Guidance from the framework: consider age, stage of dental development, and the eruption status of the permanent successor; discuss the potential need with the parent before extraction; and document whether space maintenance was recommended, deferred, or not indicated.

### 4016: Ortho and oral surgery referral checkpoints

**Statement [ALCAN]:** "I always evaluate the need for an ortho referral around 7-8 and again around 12-13 - and an oral surgery referral for 3rds around 15-16"

[ALCAN] Guidance from the framework: frame orthodontic referrals as routine developmental evaluations, not only when obvious problems exist. Monitor third molar development during adolescence, always considering the oral surgery referral around ages 15 to 16. Document referral recommendations in the record.

### 4044: Ask permission mid-appointment to complete more treatment

**Statement [ALCAN]:** "When a child is tolerating treatment well and time permits, I always ask the parent's permission mid-appointment to complete additional planned treatment the same day." *(conditionally applicable)*

[ALCAN] Guidance from the framework: assess the child's behavior, comfort, and tolerance honestly (do not push a tired child through more treatment), confirm the schedule can absorb it, ask permission and frame the savings in the family's terms, accept a decline gracefully, and align the RDA on the revised plan before continuing. Completing extra work without checking in, however well intentioned, can leave a parent feeling something was decided for them.

## Scripts and examples

All quoted from the framework's doctor script resources [ALCAN]:

**Setting the consolidation expectation (move 192):**
> "Mrs. Smith, our goal today is to complete as much as Johnny can safely and comfortably tolerate. I know we have two fillings planned today, but if he's doing great and I'm able to get more done, I always try to make the most of a good visit for you."

**Offering same-day care (move 193):**
> "To avoid another trip, we can take care of that small filling today. Give me just a few minutes to finish up some patients and set a room up and we'll get you back as soon as we can."

> "I have to finish up a couple quick hygiene checks, but if you are okay waiting about 20 minutes, I could actually take care of that filling for you guys today- know it's hard to get off work and school so I'd be happy to do that if that works for your schedule."

**Recommending the crown (move 198):**
> "For cavities in this part of the tooth, a crown is the most protective and longest-lasting option. Your child will have this tooth for another four to six years, so we want to treat it in the way that gives it the best chance of lasting."

> "A filling could technically be done, but fillings in baby teeth with this type of cavity have a much higher failure rate. Even when done well, many break or need replacement within a couple of years. Because of that, we don't feel comfortable recommending that option."

**Anterior GA transparency (move 199):**
> "When we treat front teeth under anesthesia, we plan very carefully ahead of time because we know these teeth are especially important to parents."

> "If the teeth are restorable, the most durable option is a zirconia crown. If the decay is too extensive, the most predictable option may be removing the tooth."

**Long-distance families (move 203):**
> "Since your family travels quite a way to see us, my goal is to take care of everything essential in as few visits as possible, so you're not making this drive over and over."

**IPC framing (move 204):**
> "The cavity is close to the nerve, so we will need to plan a type of nerve treatment that protects the tooth and nerve."

**Dentin lesions (move 4012):**
> "Using both the X-ray and our AI-assisted imaging, we can see that this cavity has progressed into the second layer of the tooth, called dentin."

> "Preventive options work very well when cavities are caught early in the enamel. Once decay reaches dentin, those approaches are no longer predictable on their own."

**Crown options (move 4013):**
> "We have both white zirconia crowns and traditional silver crown options. Let's talk about what matters most to you so we can decide together what is best for your child."

> "White zirconia crowns can be a little more technique-sensitive to place. If that's the option you prefer, we may recommend a higher level of sedation to make sure we can complete the treatment comfortably and with the best result for your child."

**Space maintainers (move 4014):**
> "Primary teeth help hold the correct space for the permanent teeth coming in. When one is lost early, sometimes we place a small space maintainer to keep the neighboring teeth from drifting."

**Referral checkpoints (move 4016):**
> "Around this age, I like to get orthodontic eyes on growth early. It doesn't mean treatment is needed now, but it allows a specialist to monitor how the teeth and jaws are developing."

**Mid-appointment permission (move 4044):**
> "Johnny did great for these first two crowns. Would you be okay with us continuing and completing the other side today as well? If we can save you another visit and another nitrous appointment, I think that would be great."

And when the parent declines: "Totally understand. We'll stick with today's plan and take care of the rest at the next visit."

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

[ALCAN] Sourced from the What Good Looks Like and gut check resources:

- Two quadrants of primary dentition (or one adult quadrant) completed per sedation or nitrous visit when cooperation and schedule allow, with production per patient rising over time as a practice indicator (192).
- Cancellation openings and brief schedule gaps get used for same-day procedures (193).
- Crowns are recommended confidently for the named high-failure lesion patterns, with fillings not presented as equivalent (198).
- GA anterior plans are set before surgery day, families financially and emotionally prepared (199).
- IPCs are visible in the plan for the financial team before treatment day (204).
- Every sedation dose is weight-calculated, under MRD, reduced when combined, and documented with its rationale (205).
- Space maintenance decisions are documented as recommended, deferred, or not indicated (4014).
- Referral recommendations appear in the record at the age checkpoints (4016).
- Extra same-day treatment happens only after an honest tolerance check, a schedule check, and the parent's yes (4044).

[ALCAN] Pitfalls implied by the gut checks:

- Expanding treatment when the schedule is already behind, costing other families their experience (192, 4044).
- Presenting a filling as a comparable option where crown indications are met (198).
- Offering strip crowns in the anterior GA plan (199).
- Watch-and-wait plans for families who cannot realistically return (203).
- Adding an IPC on treatment day without prior discussion, or writing it off silently (204).
- Full MRD dosing of multiple sedation medications simultaneously (205).
- Changing radiographic or restorative standards to fit insurance rather than disease (4012, 4013).
