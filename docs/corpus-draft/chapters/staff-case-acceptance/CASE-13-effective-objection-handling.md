# CASE-13 Effective Objection Handling

Role: Front Desk (DFI) | Domain: Case Acceptance | Framework code: DFI.CASE 1

## Overview

This competency belongs to the Front Desk team, also called the Director of First Impressions (DFI). [ALCAN] It is about actively preventing cancellations and unnecessary rescheduling by addressing patient concerns promptly and effectively, proactively filling open appointment slots, and, when an objection or question is beyond your scope, promptly referring the family to the right team member such as the treatment coordinator, office manager, or dentist.

[ALCAN] The framework's tagline for this one: "ABC: Always Be Closing - Adapt and overcome."

## Why it matters

[ALCAN] "You are a champion for their health. When you help a patient overcome hesitation, you aren't just filling a slot, you're ensuring they get the care they need."

[GENERAL] At the front desk you are usually the first person to hear a "no," whether it is about X-rays, cost, or an appointment itself. How you respond in that first moment often decides whether a child gets care on time. Objection handling here is not sales pressure; it is calm, confident education plus knowing exactly when to hand the conversation to someone with deeper answers.

## The moves

### Move 13: X-ray objections

[ALCAN] "I always communicate the importance of X-rays to our standard of care any time a parent objects or attempts to decline them."

[ALCAN] Parents hesitate because they care. You bridge that moment by calmly explaining that X-rays protect their child's health, helping them see care, not cost. The coaching note for this move: parents decline when they don't see value. Practice the line "X-rays let us see what our eyes can't," and ask a Lead to hear you say it so you sound confident, not pushy.

### Move 50: Insurance info up front

[ALCAN] "I always explain that having insurance info up front will contribute to a smooth visit, and I offer to wait while families gather that information."

[ALCAN] We want to take every opportunity to gather our clerical requirements. A little extra time up front saves us from scrambling or rushing when the family is with us in person. The coaching note frames it this way: it's not about money, it's about a smooth visit. Try saying "I want to make sure you don't get stuck with a bill later." It frames the request as a favor to them.

### Move 86: Cost concerns and flexible payment options

[ALCAN] "I always respond to cost concerns by highlighting all flexible payment options available."

[ALCAN] When money comes up, people tense up. Your calm, confident tone and a clear list of options turns a hard moment into a trust moment. The coaching note: cost fear is real. Don't just list prices, offer solutions. Review the CareCredit and Payment Plan talking points so you can offer them fluently.

### Move 119: Fluoride is stated, not asked

[ALCAN] "I always state that fluoride will be done and note the uncovered fee, rather than asking permission, and I direct any follow-up questions to the clinical team."

[ALCAN] Stating fluoride confidently helps avoid confusion and keeps the visit on track. You normalize the recommendation, explain the uncovered fee, and let the clinical team address any detailed questions. The coaching note: don't ask for permission, inform of the plan. Instead of "Do you want fluoride?" say "We're applying the fluoride varnish today." Confidence prevents the objection.

## Scripts and examples

All four scripts below are [ALCAN], quoted from the framework's script resources (each also has a "Script audio" recording).

**X-ray objection (move 13):**
> "I understand your concern, but X-rays help the doctor spot things we might otherwise miss, like cavities between teeth, it's part of our standard of care. If you have any specific questions or concerns about that, Dr. Alex will be able to answer all of them"

**Insurance info request (move 50):**
> "If I can grab that insurance information from you now, it'll make your visit go much smoother. I'm happy to wait while you find it."

**Cost concern (move 86):**
> "We completely understand. Many families use CareCredit, a third party financing company, to facilitate a payment plan option. Would you like more information on this?"

**Fluoride statement (move 119):**
> "The Doctor is recommending fluoride and xrays today. Your estimated patient portion for that is $30. The clinical team can walk you through any questions once you're in the back."

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

[GENERAL] Good looks like: you hear the concern fully before responding, you validate it ("I understand"), you give one clear reason tied to the child's health, and you either resolve it or name exactly who will. The family leaves the conversation feeling informed, not managed.

[GENERAL] Common pitfalls:
- Arguing the clinical case yourself instead of routing detailed questions to the doctor or clinical team.
- Asking permission for standard-of-care items (which invites a "no") instead of confidently stating the plan.
- Quoting prices without pairing them with payment options.
- Letting a hesitant family hang up or walk out without a clear next step.
