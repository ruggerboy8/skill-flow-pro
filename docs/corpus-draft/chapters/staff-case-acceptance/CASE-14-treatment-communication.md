# CASE-14 Treatment Communication

Role: Front Desk (DFI) | Domain: Case Acceptance | Framework code: DFI.CASE 2

## Overview

This competency belongs to the Front Desk (DFI) team. [ALCAN] It is about clearly and confidently answering basic patient questions about routine treatments and procedures, and, when faced with questions beyond your knowledge, proactively making sure the family still gets accurate information by providing the right resources or referring them to the correct team member, such as the treatment coordinator or dentist.

[ALCAN] The framework's tagline: "Explain or escalate."

## Why it matters

[ALCAN] "You clarify the complex. Answering questions with confidence (or knowing when to grab the doctor) empowers patients to move forward with trust."

[GENERAL] Families call the front desk with treatment questions far more often than they call the doctor. When you can answer the routine ones calmly and route the rest reliably, families stop worrying and treatment plans stay on track. The skill has two halves that matter equally: knowing what you know, and closing the loop on what you don't.

## The moves

### Move 12: NPO guidelines at confirmation

[ALCAN] "I always communicate our office's NPO guidelines for all relevant appointments when confirming."

[ALCAN] If patients haven't followed proper NPO guidelines, their appointment could need to be rescheduled. You're their last reminder to get that right. The coaching note: safety is non-negotiable. If you are fuzzy on the NPO rules, pull up the Pre-Op Checklist. Better to double-check than risk a rescheduled surgery.

### Move 49: Aftercare instructions from memory

[ALCAN] "I am able to calmly and confidently provide aftercare instructions for all common procedures."

[ALCAN] Speak like you've done it a hundred times. Calm, clear aftercare makes families feel safe, confident, and cared for long after they leave. The coaching note: you are the final voice of care. Read the Aftercare Script for fillings out loud; if you stumble, practice it one more time. Clarity prevents callbacks.

### Move 104: Close the loop on unknown questions

[ALCAN] "When a clinical question is beyond my knowledge, I create and assign a 'To-Do' in VoiceStack (phone) or CareStack (in-person), and inform the patient that someone will reach out within 24 hrs."

[ALCAN] You don't need to know everything, you just need to close the loop. Logging a To-Do and setting clear follow-up expectations shows accuracy and accountability. The coaching note: "I don't know" is a brave answer, as long as it's followed by "I'll find out." Log the To-Do immediately; if you forget to log tasks, use a sticky note buffer.

### Move 160: Benefits language, not "covered/not covered"

[ALCAN] "I always use 'estimated insurance benefits/savings' and 'patient portion/responsibility' instead of 'covered/not covered' and 'out-of-pocket'."

[ALCAN] We always refer to insurance as a benefit or a savings so there's less surprise when treatment isn't fully covered. "Out-of-pocket" gives families the mental image that we're reaching into their pocket for money.

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

[GENERAL] Good looks like: routine questions get a steady, plain-language answer on the spot; harder questions get an honest "let me get you the exact answer," a logged To-Do, and a 24-hour promise that is actually kept.

[GENERAL] Common pitfalls:
- Guessing at clinical answers to avoid saying "I don't know."
- Saying "not covered" or "out-of-pocket," which frames insurance as a betrayal instead of a benefit.
- Promising follow-up without logging it anywhere, so the loop never closes.
- Skipping the NPO reminder because the family "probably already knows."
