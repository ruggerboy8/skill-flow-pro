# CASE-30 Clear Treatment Communication

Role: Dental Assistant (RDA) | Domain: Case Acceptance | Framework code: RDA.CASE 2

## Overview

This competency belongs to Dental Assistants (RDAs). [ALCAN] It is about clearly, concisely, and confidently explaining all aspects of treatment options and plans to patients, ensuring they fully understand the procedures, benefits, and what to expect.

[ALCAN] The framework's tagline: "Explain in plain talk."

## Why it matters

[ALCAN] "You make the complex simple. Explaining treatment in plain language removes fear and empowers patients to take ownership of their smile."

[GENERAL] The moments right after the doctor leaves the chair are when a plan either becomes real for a family or dissolves into confusion. The RDA owns that window: recapping in plain words, checking how the plan lands, inviting the family to say it back, and using money language that keeps trust intact.

## The moves

### Move 27: Ask how the plan sounds

[ALCAN] "If I help explain a treatment plan, I always ask the parent 'How does that sound to you?' or 'How are you feeling about this plan?'."

[ALCAN] A gentle check-in helps parents feel heard and reassured. Asking how the plan feels opens the door for real questions before they become concerns.

### Move 64: Recap and narrate next steps after the doctor leaves

[ALCAN] "After the doctor explains the treatment and leaves the chair, I always check for comprehension and clearly narrate next steps."

[ALCAN] When the doctor steps away, parents often still have questions. A quick, friendly recap ensures understanding and builds trust in the plan. The coaching note: the unknown is scary. Tell them exactly what happens next: "We go to the front, we sign one paper, and we book for Tuesday." Roadmap the exit.

### Move 77: Invite a teach-back on the post-op plan

[ALCAN] "I always invite the guardian to share back the key parts of the post-op plan to confirm their understanding and comfort with next steps."

[ALCAN] A brief teach-back helps you catch misunderstandings early. It makes parents feel supported while ensuring their child's recovery stays on track. The coaching note: verify understanding. Ask "Just so I know I explained it right, what is the plan for dinner tonight?" (soft foods, and so on).

### Move 161: Benefits language, not "covered/not covered"

[ALCAN] "I always use 'estimated insurance benefits/savings' and 'patient portion/responsibility' instead of 'covered/not covered' and 'out-of-pocket'."

[ALCAN] We always refer to insurance as a benefit or a savings so there's less surprise when treatment isn't fully covered. "Out-of-pocket" gives families the mental image that we're reaching into their pocket for money. The coaching note: our language sets the tone. Try to catch yourself using these phrases and eliminate them from your work vocabulary.

## Scripts and examples

All scripts are [ALCAN], quoted from the framework's script resources (each with a "Script audio" recording). Doctor names are illustrative.

**Plan check-in (move 27):**
> "So, like Dr. Alex said, we'll be starting with two extractions and then finishing up with a filling. Can I ask how that plan sounds to you?"

**Post-doctor recap (move 64):**
> "Just to make sure everything feels clear, here's the plan Dr. Reyes went over: we'll take care of that small cavity with a simple filling, and doing it now keeps it from turning into something more serious. The next step is finding a time this week that works for you. Let's get you set up before you head out."

**Post-op teach-back (move 77):**
> "Just to make sure I explained everything clearly, what will tonight look like for you two? What are the top things you'll plan to do or not do after the numbing wears off?"

**Benefits language (move 161):**
> "Today we'll be completing the exam, any medically necessary radiographs, the cleaning, and protective fluoride varnish. Your estimated insurance savings are $52, and your estimated patient responsibility is $26."

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

[GENERAL] Good looks like: after every treatment conversation the family can say, in their own words, what is being done, why now, what happens tonight, and what the next appointment is. The teach-back is framed as checking your own explaining, never quizzing the parent.

[GENERAL] Common pitfalls:
- Assuming silence means understanding and skipping the check-in question.
- Recapping in clinical vocabulary instead of plain talk.
- Doing the teach-back in a way that feels like a test ("repeat that back to me").
- Slipping into "covered/not covered" or "out-of-pocket" under time pressure.

## FAQ seeds

**Q: What do I say right after the doctor leaves the chair?**
A: [ALCAN] Recap the plan in plain words, connect it to why now matters, and narrate the exact next steps, ending with scheduling before the family heads out (see the move 64 script).

**Q: How do I check understanding without making the parent feel tested?**
A: [ALCAN] Put the burden on yourself: "Just to make sure I explained everything clearly, what will tonight look like for you two?" (move 77 script).

**Q: What words do I use for insurance and costs?**
A: [ALCAN] "Estimated insurance benefits/savings" and "patient portion/responsibility." Never "covered/not covered" or "out-of-pocket."

**Q: What if the parent's answer to "how does that sound?" is negative or unsure?**
A: [ALCAN, adapted] That is the door opening, not a failure: identify the primary concern (comfort, time, or cost) and respond to the right thing, per the objection-handling competency (CASE-29). Loop in the doctor or treatment coordinator as fits the concern.
