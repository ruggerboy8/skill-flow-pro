# CASE-31 Establishing Credibility

Role: Dental Assistant (RDA) | Domain: Case Acceptance | Framework code: RDA.CASE 3

## Overview

This competency belongs to Dental Assistants (RDAs). [ALCAN] It is about building patient trust by effectively communicating the practice's and dentists' expertise, experience, and commitment to quality care, and highlighting qualifications, advanced technologies, or accolades to reassure families of the high standard of treatment they will receive.

[ALCAN] The framework's tagline: "Show we're experts."

## Why it matters

[ALCAN] "You validate their choice. Reminding patients of the team's expertise reassures them that they are receiving world-class care."

[GENERAL] Chairside, your credibility work happens in the minutes before the doctor arrives: previewing who is coming, showing off the tools on screen, and letting families picture a good outcome. A parent who trusts the room before the exam starts hears the treatment plan very differently.

## The moves

### Move 33: Preview the doctor by name

[ALCAN] "I always preview the patient's doctor by name, and comment specifically on that doctor's expertise or experience."

[ALCAN] Introducing the doctor with a quick note about their skill builds instant confidence. It helps parents feel at ease before the doctor even enters the room. The coaching note: sell the expert. "Dr. Jones is amazing at these fillings." Transfer your trust to the parent.

### Move 70: Highlight OVERJET on the radiographs

[ALCAN] "When pulling up radiographs prior to doctor's arrival, I always highlight OVERJET, and explain the benefits to the patient."

[ALCAN] By speaking confidently about the technology we use, it gives us credibility and authority. The coaching note: we're a cutting edge practice. Highlight this to make that clear.

### Move 91: Share an anonymous success story

[ALCAN] "I always share a quick, positive story about how this treatment helped another child feel better or stay healthy (no names or details), so parents can picture the real benefit."

[ALCAN] A short, anonymous success story helps parents imagine the positive outcome for their own child. It replaces fear with a clearer picture of what good care can do. The coaching note: social proof works. "We had a kiddo just last week with this same issue, and they did great." Normalize the success.

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

[GENERAL] Good looks like: the credibility beats are woven naturally into your setup routine (seat, preview the doctor, pull up images, mention OVERJET) rather than delivered as a pitch, and every claim you make is true and specific.

[GENERAL] Common pitfalls:
- Sharing a "success story" with identifying details; the framework is explicit: no names or details.
- Overpromising outcomes ("it will definitely be painless") instead of honest, positive framing.
- Reciting the OVERJET line without being able to point at what it shows on screen.
- Generic doctor praise instead of one specific, true strength.
