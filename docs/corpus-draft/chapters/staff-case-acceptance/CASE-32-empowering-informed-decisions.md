# CASE-32 Empowering Informed Decisions

Role: Dental Assistant (RDA) | Domain: Case Acceptance | Framework code: RDA.CASE 4

## Overview

This competency belongs to Dental Assistants (RDAs). [ALCAN] It is about providing comprehensive information on the clinical need for recommended treatments and the potential risks of delaying care, enabling families to make fully informed decisions about their child's oral health without feeling pressured.

[ALCAN] The framework's tagline: "Help them choose care."

## Why it matters

[ALCAN] "You empower action. By clearly outlining the 'why' behind the treatment, you help patients make the best decision for their future without feeling pressured."

[GENERAL] Informed is the opposite of pressured. A family that understands what delay actually risks, and leaves with the next visit already booked, is making a real choice rather than drifting into one. This is a small competency in move count but it carries the honest half of case acceptance: telling the truth about time.

## The moves

### Move 34: Name the risks of delay

[ALCAN] "When confirming next steps, I always highlight the health risks of delaying treatment or recare."

[ALCAN] Delays can create real risks, and parents appreciate honesty delivered with care. Explaining those risks clearly helps them make informed, confident decisions. The coaching note: this can be a gentle but firm reality check. You've seen what happens when treatment is delayed. Tell them kindly.

### Move 90: Schedule the next visit before dismissal

[ALCAN] "Before dismissal, I always ensure the next visit is scheduled at the clinically appropriate interval."

[ALCAN] Locking in the next visit keeps treatment on track. Making sure families know exactly who to reach out to prevents confusion and gives them a clear pathway for support.

## Scripts and examples

**Risk of delay (move 34), [ALCAN] from the framework script resource (with "Script audio"):**
> "If we delay this, there's a chance it could develop into something more uncomfortable. We see better results when we get it taken care of early."

Move 90 has no script resource. [GENERAL] example phrasing, drafted, not sourced:

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

[GENERAL] Good looks like: risk is explained in concrete, calm terms tied to the child's comfort and health, never as a scare tactic, and no family walks out without their next appointment booked at the interval the clinical plan calls for.

[GENERAL] Common pitfalls:
- Softening the risk message so much it disappears ("whenever you get a chance").
- Overstating risk to force urgency, which trades short-term acceptance for long-term trust.
- Letting "we'll call to schedule" substitute for booking before dismissal.
- Booking the next visit at whatever slot is convenient rather than the clinically appropriate interval.
