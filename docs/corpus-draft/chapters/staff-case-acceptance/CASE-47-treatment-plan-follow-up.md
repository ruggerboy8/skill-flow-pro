# CASE-47 Treatment Plan Follow-Up

Role: Office Manager | Domain: Case Acceptance

This is an honest stub. The framework carries two pro moves here, no competency description, and no script resources, so a good share of this chapter is well-formed gaps.

## Overview

This competency belongs to Office Managers. [ALCAN] The framework's tagline is "Don't let care slip away," and its friendly description: "You maintain systems that track unscheduled or incomplete treatment and drive timely follow-up so important care doesn't fall through the cracks."

## Why it matters

[GENERAL] Case acceptance doesn't end when a family says yes. Between acceptance and treatment sits insurance paperwork, and every day it stalls is a day a child waits. This competency is the office manager's ownership of that pipeline.

[ALCAN] Submitting preauthorizations within 48 hours speeds up insurance decisions and keeps treatment moving forward without long delays; checking approvals daily ensures you don't miss a green light from insurance or let stalled cases sit without action.

## The moves

### Move 186: Preauth documentation within 48 hours

[ALCAN] "I always review and submit all preauthorization documentation no more than 48 hours after treatment acceptance."

[ALCAN] The framework's practice note: after a case is accepted, make it a rule to start and finish preauths within the next two working days.

### Move 187: Review preauth approvals daily

[ALCAN] "I always review preauthorization approvals every day and follow up with insurance as necessary."

[ALCAN] The framework's practice note: add preauth approvals to your daily checklist and follow up on anything pending or unclear.

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

[GENERAL] Good looks like: a visible pipeline where every accepted case has a preauth submitted within two working days, every pending preauth has a last-checked date no older than yesterday, and approvals convert to scheduled appointments the day they arrive.

[GENERAL] Common pitfalls: batching preauth submissions weekly; treating "submitted" as done and never chasing pending ones; approvals arriving and sitting unnoticed while the family loses momentum.
