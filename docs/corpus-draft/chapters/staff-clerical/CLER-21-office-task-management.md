# CLER-21: Office Task Management

**Role:** Dental Assistant (RDA) | **Domain:** Clerical | **Competency:** 21 (RDA.CLER 1) | **Tagline:** [ALCAN] "Stock & fix stuff"

## Overview

This competency covers the RDA's non-clinical housekeeping: supplies, equipment upkeep, compliance tasks, and using downtime productively. [ALCAN] The framework defines it as effectively handling "non-clinical administrative tasks such as inventory management, ordering supplies, and coordinating equipment maintenance," contributing "to a well-organized and smoothly functioning dental practice by completing administrative duties accurately and on time."

It applies to everyone in the Dental Assistant (RDA) role.

## Why it matters

[ALCAN] From the framework: "A great practice runs on details. Managing inventory and equipment behind the scenes guarantees the clinical team never has to hit pause."

[GENERAL] Running out of gauze mid-procedure, an autoclave log that was never checked, an empty afternoon column that nobody tried to fill: these are the quiet failures that make a clinical day feel chaotic. This competency is the RDA's share of keeping the machine running between patients.

## The moves

### 26. Clear Uptime before you leave, every day

[ALCAN] "I check Uptime and complete outstanding tasks prior to departure every day."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Finishing Uptime tasks daily keeps the practice safe, compliant, and ready for the next day. Consistent follow-through prevents surprises and protects patient care." And: "Don't leave it for the closer. If you have 5 minutes, wipe a counter or stock a drawer. Small acts now prevent late nights later."

### 63. Use 45-minute gaps to work the Reschedule list

[ALCAN] "When I have 45 minutes or more between patients I always use that time to send messages to the Reschedule list to help fill the schedule."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Small pockets of time add up. Using those moments to reach out to the Reschedule list keeps the day efficient and helps patients stay on track with needed care."

### 97. Check high-turnover disposables twice a day

[ALCAN] "Before lunch and at the end of the day, I always check high-turnover disposables and note anything running low so we never start short."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Quick supply checks prevent mid-day shortages. Catching low items early keeps procedures running smoothly and protects the pace of care." And: "No surprises. Check the high-volume items (suction tips, gauze) before the afternoon rush. Running out mid-procedure is preventable."

### 128. Be ready for any assigned Uptime maintenance or compliance task

[ALCAN] "I'm always prepared to perform any maintenance or compliance task listed in the Uptime checklist when assigned."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Staying ready for maintenance tasks keeps the office safe and compliant. Following the checklist consistently supports the entire team's workflow." And: "Consistency is safety. Don't skip the boring checks. Verify the O2 tank or the autoclave logs. It matters."

### 156. Book the 6-month recare while the patient is in the chair

[ALCAN] "I always schedule the 6-month recare appointment while the patient is in-chair."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Keeping our patients in a consistent flow is crucial, and you're in the best position to make sure this gets done every time." And: "Don't rely on the front desk. The best time to rebook is when the patient is happy in the chair. Say: Let's secure your spot for 6 months so you get the time you want."

## Scripts and examples

**Booking the 6-month recare in-chair (move 156)**, from the framework's script resource for this move:

> [ALCAN] "Ok, before you get out of here, we'll get your next appointment on the books. 6 months from now puts us into July. Tell me what day of the week you have the most control over your schedule, and I'll find some possible times for us."

[GENERAL] Example phrasing for a Reschedule list message: "Hi! We noticed [child]'s appointment needed a new time. We'd love to get that back on the schedule. Would this week or next work better for you?"

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

[GENERAL] **What good looks like:**
- Uptime shows zero outstanding tasks when you walk out the door.
- Supply checks happen before lunch and at close, so shortages are caught while there is still time to act.
- Long gaps between patients turn into schedule-filling outreach, not idle time.
- Nearly every recare patient leaves with their next appointment already booked.

[GENERAL] **Common pitfalls:**
- Assuming "the closer" will handle Uptime, so tasks pile onto whoever leaves last.
- Noticing a low supply mid-procedure instead of during the twice-daily check.
- Handing recare booking off to the front desk at checkout, where it competes with phones and payments and often gets lost.
- Treating compliance checks (O2 tank, autoclave logs) as optional busywork.
