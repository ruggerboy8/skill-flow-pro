# CLER-22: Coordinating Patient Flow

**Role:** Dental Assistant (RDA) | **Domain:** Clerical | **Competency:** 22 (RDA.CLER 2) | **Tagline:** [ALCAN] "Move 'em smoothly"

## Overview

This competency covers the RDA's role in keeping patients moving through the clinical day. [ALCAN] The framework defines it as ensuring "a seamless flow of patients through the practice by efficiently coordinating with the dental team and front office staff," minimizing "wait times" and enhancing "the patient experience by effectively managing appointment schedules and promptly addressing any delays or scheduling conflicts."

It applies to everyone in the Dental Assistant (RDA) role.

## Why it matters

[ALCAN] From the framework: "You keep the rhythm of the back office going. Coordinating with the front desk minimizes wait times and respects everyone's schedule."

[GENERAL] The front desk can only see what the software shows, and the doctor can only be where someone directs them. The RDA sits at the hinge between the two. Accurate statuses, early warnings about delays, clear directions for the doctor, and a steady personal pace are what keep one late appointment from cascading into a late day.

## The moves

### 23. Update patient status immediately

[ALCAN] "I always immediately change patient status to 'In Chair' when they are seated and 'Treatment Complete' when they are ready to be checked out."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Updating status right away keeps the whole team coordinated. It helps the doctor see who's ready and keeps families from waiting longer than necessary." And: "The status runs the board. If you don't click In Chair, the front desk thinks the room is empty. Update it the second they sit down."

### 60. Ten minutes late? Check with the Lead RDA first

[ALCAN] "When I notice that I'm starting an appointment more than 10 minutes late, I check with the Lead RDA before starting so that I understand the plan to get back on track."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Time warnings help you stay ahead of delays. Looping in the lead quickly keeps the day flowing and prevents small issues from becoming big bottlenecks."

### 129. Actively manage doctor flow

[ALCAN] "I always actively manage doctor flow by clearly communicating when and where patients are ready, including location, procedure, and patient type, to keep the schedule running smoothly."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Clear, proactive updates keep the doctor moving efficiently. Sharing the right details at the right moment prevents confusion and helps the entire day run smoothly." And: "Be the traffic controller. Tell the doctor exactly where to go next. Room 2 is ready for numbing. Direct traffic to save time."

### 130. Same pace, calm or hectic

[ALCAN] "I always maintain the same professional pace and focus whether the office is calm or hectic."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "A steady pace builds trust with both patients and the team. Staying consistent, even when things get busy, helps the day feel organized instead of chaotic." And: "Calm adds speed. Rushing creates mistakes. Take one deep breath. Move with purpose, not panic. The patient mirrors your energy."

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

[GENERAL] **What good looks like:**
- The schedule board always matches reality, because statuses change the second the patient sits or finishes.
- Delays over 10 minutes trigger a quick huddle with the Lead RDA before the appointment starts, not an apology after.
- The doctor never has to wander looking for the next patient; they are told where to go, for what, and for whom.
- Your walking pace and tone are the same at 4 pm on a slammed day as at 8 am on a quiet one.

[GENERAL] **Common pitfalls:**
- Batching status updates "when I get a second," which leaves the front desk seating decisions blind.
- Absorbing a delay silently and starting late without a plan, pushing the whole column back.
- Giving the doctor vague updates ("someone's ready") that force follow-up questions.
- Visibly rushing when it gets busy, which rattles patients and multiplies mistakes.
