# CLER-23: Patient Record Management

**Role:** Dental Assistant (RDA) | **Domain:** Clerical | **Competency:** 23 (RDA.CLER 3) | **Tagline:** [ALCAN] "Records airtight"

## Overview

This competency covers the RDA's documentation duties around each visit. [ALCAN] The framework defines it as maintaining "accurate, up-to-date patient records by applying knowledge of record-keeping protocols and confidentiality requirements," ensuring "all documentation, including medical histories, treatment notes, and consent forms, is completed correctly and securely, complying with legal and ethical standards."

It applies to everyone in the Dental Assistant (RDA) role.

## Why it matters

[ALCAN] From the framework: "Accuracy is your hallmark. Maintaining airtight patient records protects the practice and ensures the highest standard of care continuity."

[GENERAL] In pediatric dentistry the stakes on this are concrete: medication dosing runs off recorded weight, treatment decisions run off history and photos, and the clinical note is a legal document. The moves here bracket the visit: prepare before it (memo and history), measure accurately at the start (height and weight), and document before it ends (photo and notes).

## The moves

### 21. Check the memo and medical history before starting

[ALCAN] "I always check the patient memo and ensure a complete medical history before beginning the appointment."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Reviewing memos and history before you start keeps everyone safe and prevents surprises. It helps you walk into the room prepared and confident." And: "Never fly blind. Read the Medical Alerts out loud to yourself before the patient sits down. If something looks odd, ask the Doctor."

### 58. Enter height and weight accurately, double-checked

[ALCAN] "I always accurately enter patient's height and weight in the proper units and double check for errors before seating them."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Accurate measurements matter for safe dosing and charting. Double-checking helps you avoid mistakes and keeps the appointment on track." And, on why this is non-negotiable: "Dosage depends on this. If the scale looks off, re-weigh them. Accuracy here prevents medication errors."

### 123. Attach an intraoral photo after each applicable procedure

[ALCAN] "After each applicable procedure, I always attach one intraoral photo to support patient understanding and insurance documentation."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "A clear photo helps parents understand the procedure and strengthens documentation for the doctor and insurance. It's a quick step that adds clarity for everyone." Struggling with it? "A picture justifies the claim. If you struggle to get good angles, grab a Lead RDA between patients and practice your camera positioning."

### 124. Draft clinical notes before the appointment ends

[ALCAN] "I always complete and save clinical notes as a draft before the end of each appointment."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Complete notes keep the entire care team aligned. Finishing them before the appointment ends ensures accuracy and prevents details from being forgotten." And: "Notes are legal documents. Don't wait until lunch. Finish the draft before you dismiss the patient, while the details are fresh."

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

[GENERAL] **What good looks like:**
- You could summarize the patient's alerts and memo from memory before they sit down.
- Height and weight are entered in the correct units and sanity-checked against the child in front of you.
- Applicable procedures each have a clear intraoral photo attached before the day moves on.
- The note draft is saved before the patient is dismissed, every time.

[GENERAL] **Common pitfalls:**
- Skimming the memo instead of reading the alerts carefully; the framework's habit is to read Medical Alerts out loud to yourself.
- Unit mistakes (pounds vs kilograms) or typos that flow silently into dosing math.
- Deferring notes to lunch or end of day, when details have blurred and appointments have stacked.
- Skipping the photo when running behind, weakening both parent understanding and the insurance claim.
