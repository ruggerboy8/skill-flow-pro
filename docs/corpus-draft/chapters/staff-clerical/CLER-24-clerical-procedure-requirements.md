# CLER-24: Clerical Procedure Requirements

**Role:** Dental Assistant (RDA) | **Domain:** Clerical | **Competency:** 24 (RDA.CLER 4) | **Tagline:** [ALCAN] "Paperwork done right"

## Overview

This competency covers the compliance checks an RDA owns around each procedure. [ALCAN] The framework defines it as ensuring "that all clerical tasks related to patient procedures, such as obtaining consent forms, verifying insurance information, and scheduling follow-up appointments, are completed accurately and promptly," collaborating "with the clinical team to support seamless patient care through clear communication and timely documentation."

It applies to everyone in the Dental Assistant (RDA) role. [ALCAN] The framework's own self-check question for this competency: "How consistently do you ensure that consent forms are signed, the guardian is authorized, and x-rays are properly labelled?"

## Why it matters

[ALCAN] From the framework: "You close the loop on care. Handling consents and follow-ups promptly means the patient's journey is complete and compliant."

[GENERAL] These three moves are the legal guardrails of the clinical day. Treating a minor without a signed consent, discussing health information with an unauthorized adult, or diagnosing from an unlabeled image are each serious failures, and each is fully preventable with a check that takes under a minute. This is a short competency because the standard is simple: no check, no procedure.

## The moves

### 29. Verify consent forms before beginning

[ALCAN] "I always verify that proper consent forms have been completed prior to beginning the appointment."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Confirming consents protects both the patient and the practice. A quick check at the start ensures care begins safely and legally." The line to hold: "No consent, no treatment. If the signature is missing, do not recline the chair. Ask the parent to sign on the iPad immediately."

### 66. Label teeth in x-ray images before leaving the workstation

[ALCAN] "After taking an x-ray I always label the teeth in the images before leaving the workstation."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Labeling images immediately keeps records clear and helps the doctor diagnose without delay. It also prevents mix-ups later in the day." And: "Data without labels is useless. Tag the teeth numbers immediately after exposure. Unlabeled mounts confuse the whole clinical team."

### 78. Verify the adult is on the Authorized Individual Form

[ALCAN] "For all patients, I always verify that the adult with them is listed on the Authorized Individual Form before beginning the appointment."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Checking authorization keeps every interaction safe and compliant. This quick verification protects patients, families, and the practice." And: "Protect the minor. If Grandma brings the child, check the Authorized list before you talk about medical history. HIPAA matters."

[GENERAL] Note that the front desk performs a matching check at check-in (see CLER-5, move 164). The RDA's check is the second layer, before any health information is discussed in the back.

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

[GENERAL] **What good looks like:**
- The chair never reclines without a completed consent on file.
- Every image leaves the x-ray workstation already labeled with tooth numbers.
- The authorization check happens before any medical history conversation, for every patient, no matter how familiar the adult seems.

[GENERAL] **Common pitfalls:**
- Assuming the front desk already handled consent or authorization, so nobody actually checked.
- "I'll label those after lunch," which is exactly how images get mixed up.
- Skipping the authorization check for a friendly face who has been in before but was never added to the form.
