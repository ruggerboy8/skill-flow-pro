# CLER-39: Root Cause Analysis

**Role:** Office Manager | **Domain:** Clerical | **Competency:** 39 | **Tagline:** [ALCAN] "Look past symptoms, fix drivers"

## Overview

This is a thin section: the framework carries one pro move for it and no long-form competency description. [ALCAN] Its friendly description: "You use data and patterns to dig past surface issues, find the real cause, and lead changes that stop problems from repeating."

## Why it matters

[GENERAL] Working the same billing error every week is a treadmill; finding why it keeps happening and changing the behavior behind it is the only exit. The single move here applies that mindset to claim flags, but the pattern (spot the recurrence, teach the why, watch the trend) is the general shape of the competency.

## The moves

### 175. Weekly claim-flag pattern analysis, taught at huddle, tracked over time

[ALCAN] "I always analyze common claim flags at least once a week and communicate specific behavior changes to the team during huddle, then monitor whether those flags decrease over time."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Looking at patterns in claim flags and changing behavior prevents the same billing mistakes from happening over and over again." Tip: "Choose one or two recurring flag types each week, share the 'why' in huddle, and track if those flags drop."

[GENERAL] The move is a full loop, not a report: analyze, translate into a specific behavior change, communicate it at huddle, then verify the flags actually decrease. If they do not drop, the diagnosis was wrong and the loop runs again.

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

[GENERAL] **What good looks like:**
- One or two flag types get real attention each week, with a named behavior change, instead of a vague "be more careful."
- Last week's flag counts come back to huddle, so the team sees whether the change worked.

[GENERAL] **Common pitfalls:**
- Fixing individual flagged claims without ever asking why the same flag keeps appearing.
- Announcing a change once and never checking the trend, so nobody knows if it helped.
