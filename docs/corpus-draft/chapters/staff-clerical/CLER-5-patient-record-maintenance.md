# CLER-5: Patient Record Maintenance

**Role:** Front Desk (DFI) | **Domain:** Clerical | **Competency:** 5 (DFI.CLER 1) | **Tagline:** [ALCAN] "Perfect patient data"

## Overview

This competency covers how the front desk keeps patient records complete and accurate. [ALCAN] The framework defines it as keeping "all patient records up-to-date and accurate, including treatments, notes, medical history, consents, referral documentation, and insurance/financial documents," so that "all records the front desk handles are meticulously maintained for seamless patient care and compliance."

It applies to everyone in the Front Desk (DFI) role. DFI means Director of First Impressions, which is the same role as Front Desk.

## Why it matters

[ALCAN] The framework puts it this way: "Great care starts with great data. Your meticulous attention to patient records ensures nothing gets missed and every visit is safe and seamless."

[GENERAL] In a pediatric practice, the record the front desk hands to the clinical team is the foundation for everything that follows: safe treatment, correct billing, and legal protection for the child, the family, and the practice. A missing medical history or an unauthorized adult at check-in is not a paperwork problem, it is a safety and compliance problem. The moves in this competency exist so those problems get caught at the desk, before they reach the chair.

## The moves

### 2. Check paperwork before changing patient status

[ALCAN] "I always check for a complete medical history and required forms for the visit before changing patient status to either 'Checked In' OR 'Arrived - No Paperwork'."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Don't mark them 'checked in' until the forms are done. It protects the clinical team's time and keeps the visit running clean." And when it slips: "A checked box protects a life. Never auto-pilot this. If the form isn't signed, the patient isn't Ready. Hold the line."

[GENERAL] The status field is a promise to the clinical team. "Checked In" should mean genuinely ready to be pulled back, forms and all. Using "Arrived - No Paperwork" honestly is better than a hopeful "Checked In."

### 39. Confirm insurance verification before completing check-in

[ALCAN] "I always confirm insurance verification status prior to completing check-in."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Confirm insurance before you close the chart. It saves headaches later and tells families you've already handled the details." If you are unsure whether a plan is active: "Verification is the gatekeeper. If you aren't sure if a plan is active, ask the Insurance Coordinator to verify it with you right now."

### 100. Work the "Incomplete Paperwork" list daily

[ALCAN] "I set aside time daily to call or text people on the 'Incomplete Paperwork' list in order to keep same-day verifications below 3%."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Keep paperwork from becoming panic work. A quick call or text today prevents tomorrow's bottleneck." A concrete nudge from the framework: "Chasing forms is better than stalling the clinic. Send 3 texts right now to tomorrow's Incomplete list. Future-you will thank you."

[GENERAL] The habit here is proactive outreach: a small, protected block of time each day beats a scramble at tomorrow's check-in.

### 164. Confirm the adult's preferred name, relationship, and authorization

[ALCAN] "I always begin my check in process by confirming the preferred name and relationship for the person who has brought the patient (i.e. mom, aunt, grandma), and checking to ensure they are authorized."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "This first matters for HIPAA compliance, but also because it's valuable for us to know exactly what folks like to be called!" And: "Don't forget this, it's CRUCIAL that we make sure patients are with someone approved to bring them in for treatment. It never hurts to ask!"

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

[GENERAL] **What good looks like:**
- Every "Checked In" status genuinely means forms complete and insurance verified.
- The Incomplete Paperwork list is touched every day, not just when it is convenient.
- Every check-in opens by confirming who the adult is, what they like to be called, and that they are authorized.

[GENERAL] **Common pitfalls:**
- Marking a family "Checked In" to keep the lobby number looking good while forms are still outstanding.
- Assuming the adult is a parent instead of asking, which risks both a HIPAA issue and an awkward moment.
- Treating paperwork outreach as a when-there-is-time task, so same-day verifications pile up.

## FAQ seeds

**Q: The family is here and the medical history is not complete. Do I check them in anyway?**
A: [ALCAN] No. The framework is explicit: if the form isn't signed, the patient isn't ready. Use the "Arrived - No Paperwork" status instead and get the forms completed before changing to "Checked In."

**Q: What if I'm not sure the insurance plan is active?**
A: [ALCAN] Ask the Insurance Coordinator to verify it with you right now, before completing check-in.

**Q: The adult with the child is not on the authorization list. What exactly do I do?**
A: [GAP] The framework says to verify authorization but does not define the escalation path when the adult is not authorized.

**Q: What counts toward the "same-day verifications below 3%" target, and where do I see that number?**
A: [GAP] The framework names the target but not how it is measured or where it is reported.

**Q: Which forms are "required forms for the visit" for each appointment type?**
A: [GAP] The framework does not include the form list by visit type.
