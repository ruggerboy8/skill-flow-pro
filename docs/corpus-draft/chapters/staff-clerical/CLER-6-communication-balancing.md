# CLER-6: Communication Balancing

**Role:** Front Desk (DFI) | **Domain:** Clerical | **Competency:** 6 (DFI.CLER 2) | **Tagline:** [ALCAN] "Nothing falls through the cracks"

## Overview

This competency is about juggling every channel the front desk owns: the person standing at the desk, the ringing phone, texts, emails, and voicemail. [ALCAN] The framework defines it as skillfully balancing "various communication demands throughout the day, consistently managing in-person greetings, incoming calls, emails, and messages" and maintaining "smooth operations even during high-traffic times by prioritizing tasks and providing attentive service to patients and team members."

It applies to everyone in the Front Desk (DFI) role.

## Why it matters

[ALCAN] From the framework: "You are the master of multitasking. Balancing phones, emails, and smiles means every patient feels heard and the practice never misses a beat."

[GENERAL] Families judge a practice by how quickly and warmly someone responds. Every unanswered ring, ignored walk-in, or stale voicemail quietly says "you are not a priority." The moves in this competency turn responsiveness from a personality trait into a routine with concrete standards.

## The moves

### 3. Finish check-in with hospitality before returning to other tasks

[ALCAN] "I always fully complete the check in process with an offer of hospitality and introduction to the play area before returning to other tasks."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Greet, welcome, and finish the moment. Hospitality first, multitasking second, it's how families know they matter here." And: "Don't leave them hanging. The Play Area Handoff is the period at the end of the sentence. Practice transitioning from paperwork to welcome today."

### 40. The 3-3-3 responsiveness standard

[ALCAN] "I always answer phones within 3 rings, respond to texts within 3 minutes, and return emails within 3 hours."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Fast replies build confidence. When families know you're responsive, they stop worrying if anyone's paying attention." If you find yourself missing these targets: "Responsiveness is our brand. If you are missing these targets, you might be multitasking too much. Ask a colleague to watch your workflow for 10 mins and help spot the bottleneck."

### 80. Clear the VoiceStack To-Do list every day

[ALCAN] "I always complete all items in the VoiceStack 'To-Do' list before the end of the day every day."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Finish what you start. A clear To-Do list means clean communication, no loose ends, and zero 'Did anyone handle that?' moments." Feeling behind? "Clear the deck. Leaving tasks for tomorrow creates a debt that slows you down. Knock out the 3 easiest tasks on your list right now."

### 150. Serve the patient in front of you, kindly hold the caller

[ALCAN] "When I am on the phone and a patient walks in, I find the soonest natural break and kindly place them on a brief hold in order to serve the patient in front of me."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Balancing in-person and phone communication takes awareness. Stepping away at a natural pause lets you greet walk-ins warmly without leaving your caller hanging. Both patients feel seen, not sidelined." And: "The person in front of you wins. It feels rude to interrupt the caller, but it's worse to ignore the lobby. Practice the phrase: I have a patient walking in, may I place you on a brief hold?"

### 151. Sweep voicemail and missed calls three times a day

[ALCAN] "I always check the voicemails / missed calls on VoiceStack in the AM, at lunch, and in the PM and take action as necessary."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Checking missed calls throughout the day keeps communication tight and prevents families from feeling ignored. A quick sweep in the morning, at lunch, and before closing helps make sure no one slips through the cracks." And: "The AM/Lunch/PM rhythm is non-negotiable. If you skip the Lunch check, the PM pile becomes unmanageable. Set a phone alarm for just before Lunch to clear the board."

> [ALCAN] "Thank you, now, if you'll give me one quick moment, I'm going to place you on a brief hold and then I'll be right back with you."

[GENERAL] Example phrasing when returning to the caller: "Thanks so much for holding. Now, where were we?"

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

[GENERAL] **What good looks like:**
- Every walk-in gets a warm, complete welcome, and callers are held briefly and kindly rather than ignored.
- Phones answered within 3 rings, texts within 3 minutes, emails within 3 hours, consistently, not just on slow days.
- Voicemail is swept morning, lunch, and end of day, and the VoiceStack To-Do list goes home empty.

[GENERAL] **Common pitfalls:**
- Half-finishing a check-in to grab the phone, leaving the family unsure whether they are done.
- Letting the caller monopolize you while a family stands at the desk unacknowledged.
- Skipping the midday voicemail sweep, so the afternoon pile becomes unmanageable.
- Rolling To-Do items to "tomorrow" until they turn into missed follow-ups.
