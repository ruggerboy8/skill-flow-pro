# CLER-8: Welcoming Environment

**Role:** Front Desk (DFI) | **Domain:** Clerical | **Competency:** 8 (DFI.CLER 4) | **Tagline:** [ALCAN] "Reception always ready"

## Overview

This competency covers the physical front-of-house: keeping reception clean, stocked, and comfortable. [ALCAN] The framework defines it as maintaining "a clean, organized, and inviting front desk and reception area," ensuring "supplies are stocked, spaces are tidy, and the environment is comfortable, creating a positive first impression for patients."

It applies to everyone in the Front Desk (DFI) role.

## Why it matters

[ALCAN] From the framework: "First impressions happen here. By keeping the reception area welcoming and organized, you tell every patient, 'We care about excellence.'"

[GENERAL] Parents read the waiting room as a proxy for the clinical care they cannot see. A tidy lobby, a clean bathroom, and calm music say "we sweat the details" before anyone says a word. This is a small competency with only three moves, but they run on a clock: hourly, three times a day, and continuously.

## The moves

### 5. The hourly reset

[ALCAN] "I always tidy the reception area, check the patient bathroom, and restock amenities in the front area once every hour."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Keep the front fresh. A quick reset each hour shows pride in your space and helps every family feel like the first one of the day." A great self-check from the framework: "You get used to the mess. Reset your eyes. Walk out the front door and walk back in. What is the first thing you notice? Fix it."

### 42. Vacuum three times a day

[ALCAN] "I always thoroughly vacuum the front area in the morning, before leaving for lunch, and at the end of the day."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Every detail families see tells a story. A spotless front area says, 'we care about our space, and about you.'" If this slips: "A clean floor signals a clean clinic. If this slips, check your Closing Checklist routine. Do you need to start 10 minutes earlier?"

### 107. Keep the right music at the right level

[ALCAN] "I always check throughout the day to make sure the correct music is playing at an appropriate level."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "The sound of the office shapes how people feel. Keep it calm, friendly, and at a volume that lets conversation breathe." And: "Vibes matter. If the music is too loud or too sleepy, change it. You are the DJ of the patient experience."

## Scripts and examples

No script resources exist in the framework for this competency's moves, and none are really needed; these are environment habits, not conversations.

[GENERAL] A practical pattern: tie the hourly reset to something that already happens every hour (the top of the hour on the schedule board, or right after a wave of check-ins) so it becomes automatic rather than remembered.

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

[GENERAL] **What good looks like:**
- A parent arriving at 4 pm sees the same lobby a parent saw at 8 am: tidy, stocked, clean bathroom, calm music.
- The reset happens on a rhythm, not in reaction to a mess someone noticed.
- The walk-in-the-front-door test comes up clean: nothing jars you in the first three seconds.

[GENERAL] **Common pitfalls:**
- Going "mess blind" from sitting in the space all day; the framework's fix is to walk out and walk back in with fresh eyes.
- Skipping the lunch vacuum on busy days, exactly when the floor needs it most.
- Music drifting: too loud after a volume bump for one family, or a playlist that wandered off-brand and nobody noticed.
