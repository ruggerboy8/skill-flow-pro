## Overview

[ALCAN] This competency is about ensuring that all equipment, instruments, and treatment rooms are thoroughly sterilized, properly stocked, and correctly set up for each specific procedure, following established protocols to maintain a safe and efficient clinical environment.

It applies to Dental Assistants (RDAs). The framework's framing: "Safety starts with you. By maintaining a pristine clinical environment, you protect every patient and set the stage for flawless procedures."

Important scope note: this chapter covers the Pro Moves habits around sterilization and prep. It is **not** a sterilization protocol. ## Why it matters

[ALCAN] The framework connects these habits to both safety and trust. "Cross-contamination happens when we rush." And visibly clean matters as much as technically clean: "Visibility is trust. If a parent sees a used rag, they doubt the sterile instruments." On preparation: "Scrambling mid-procedure causes stress," while a full reset "keeps every patient safe and every provider set up for success."

[GENERAL] In pediatric dentistry, parents are watching the room as closely as the care. Consistent glove discipline, visible fresh supplies, and a complete room reset protect patients clinically and protect the practice's credibility at the same time.

## The moves

### Move 19: Glove discipline

[ALCAN] "I always use gloves only when handling contaminated instruments or chemicals, and remove them before touching anything clean or sterile."

[ALCAN] Clean and sterile items stay safe when gloves are used only for what is dirty. This habit prevents cross-contamination and protects both patients and the clinical team. The framework's coaching step: pause and visualize your Clean to Dirty flow, and ask a Lead RDA to audit your next room turnover.

### Move 56: Set up the tray, plus likely alternatives, before seating

[ALCAN] "I always set up the tray for the scheduled procedure and any likely alternatives before bringing the patient back."

[ALCAN] Preparing the full tray in advance keeps appointments smooth and prevents scrambling once the patient is seated. Set up everything, including the "just in case" items, before the patient enters, and check your setup against the visual guide.

### Move 102: Run the full room reset checklist

[ALCAN] "I always review the entire room reset checklist and confirm every item is complete before considering the room ready for the next patient."

[ALCAN] A complete reset keeps every patient safe and every provider set up for success. "Don't trust your memory; trust the checklist." One missed item can delay the next appointment; physically touch the checklist for your next turnover.

### Move 120: Fresh cleaning rags for every patient

[ALCAN] "I always use fresh cleaning rags for each individual patient encounter."

[ALCAN] Using a new rag for every patient protects against cross-contamination and keeps the room visibly clean. No exceptions.

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

[GENERAL] What good looks like:

- Anyone watching a turnover could narrate which hands are "dirty" and which are "clean" at every moment.
- The tray for the scheduled procedure, plus the just-in-case items, is complete before the patient walks in, so nobody leaves the room mid-procedure.
- The checklist is physically used every turnover, not recited from memory.
- A parent scanning the room sees nothing that makes them wonder.

[GENERAL] Common pitfalls:

- Keeping gloves on "just for a second" to grab something clean.
- Setting up only for the scheduled procedure and betting against surprises.
- Running the checklist from memory on busy days, which is exactly when items get skipped.
- Reusing a rag that "still looks fine."
