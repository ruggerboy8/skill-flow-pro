## Overview

[ALCAN] This competency is about demonstrating exceptional knowledge and skill in performing all dental procedures, both independently and when assisting the dentist, executing each step with precision and appropriate timing to deliver high-quality patient care.

It applies to Dental Assistants (RDAs). The framework's framing: "You are the dentist's right hand. Executing procedures with precision and anticipating needs makes the appointment smoother for everyone."

## Why it matters

[ALCAN] Both moves target the same outcome: the doctor never waits and the patient never waits twice. "A bad X-ray wastes the Doctor's time during the exam," and good assisting "feels almost choreographed," creating "a seamless experience for the patient."

[GENERAL] With children in the chair, wasted minutes are not neutral. Every retake, every pause to hunt for an instrument, extends the time a child has to hold still and hold it together. Precision assisting is a comfort tool as much as an efficiency tool.

## The moves

### Move 25: Verify X-rays before releasing the patient from the machine

[ALCAN] "I always verify that my Xrays are usable for the doctor before moving the patient from the Xray machine."

[ALCAN] Checking the image right away saves time and prevents repeat exposures. Ensuring clarity helps the doctor diagnose accurately and keeps the visit moving. "Don't guess on shadows. If the apex isn't clear, retake it immediately."

[GENERAL] The moment to catch a bad image is while the patient is still positioned; a retake then costs seconds, a retake discovered during the exam costs the whole room's momentum plus a second trip for the child.

### Move 62: Anticipate the next instrument

[ALCAN] "I always anticipate the next instrument the doctor will need and have it in hand before the doctor asks for it."

[ALCAN] Smooth assisting feels almost choreographed. "Stay one step ahead. Watch the Doctor's eyes, not just their hands. If they look at the composite, have the curing light ready."

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

[GENERAL] What good looks like:

- The patient leaves the X-ray station exactly once per image series.
- Instrument passes happen without the doctor asking out loud; the appointment sounds quiet and looks choreographed.
- The RDA can read the procedure's rhythm well enough to know what comes next in each sequence they assist.

[GENERAL] Common pitfalls:

- Accepting a marginal image and hoping the doctor can work with it.
- Watching the current step instead of preparing the next one.
- Confusing speed with anticipation; rushing passes is not the same as being ready.
