# CLIN-19 Accurate Charting

Role: Dental Assistant (RDA) · Framework code: RDA.CLIN 3 · Tagline: "Chart like a pro"

## Overview

[ALCAN] This competency is about demonstrating comprehensive knowledge of dental terminology, oral anatomy, and practice management software to accurately and thoroughly record patient information and complete clinical notes, converting the dentist's verbal instructions into accurate charting and treatment planning.

It applies to Dental Assistants (RDAs). The framework's framing: "You write the history of their care. Accurate, professional charting ensures that every future decision is based on the full picture."

This chapter pairs with the doctor-side chapter CLIN-401 (Chart Accuracy): the doctor's moves there are to verbalize findings clearly and completely; the RDA's moves here are to capture them precisely. Charting at Alcan is designed as a two-person system.

## Why it matters

[ALCAN] The framework's through-line is that charting errors compound downstream. "Accuracy is a team sport... Catch the error now, not at billing." Small wording differences carry big consequences: "Charting Watch vs Treat changes the entire treatment plan." And on documenting the family's decisions: "Capture the agreement... Don't rely on memory." Precise notes "prevent confusion and keep the patient journey smooth" across scheduling, billing, and follow-up care.

[GENERAL] The chart is the single artifact that outlives the appointment. Insurance, the next provider, and the family's future treatment decisions all depend on what got recorded in those few minutes.

## The moves

### Move 65: Record findings accurately as the doctor calls them out

[ALCAN] "As the doctor calls out findings, I actively listen and accurately record correct conditions on correct teeth (both in note and charted on the odontogram)."

[ALCAN] Accurate charting helps the whole team deliver safe, consistent care. Listen for the code, and if the doctor mumbles, ask them to repeat. The gap between "watch" and "treat" is the gap between two different treatment plans.

### Move 76: Record the treatment the family actually agreed to

[ALCAN] "As the doctor presents treatment options, I always actively listen, and accurately record all mutually agreed upon treatment."

[ALCAN] Documenting agreed treatment clearly supports scheduling, billing, and follow-up care. If the parent agrees to the sealant but declines the fluoride, mark it immediately; do not rely on memory.

### Move 28: Get a doctor review before they leave the chair

[ALCAN] "I always ask the doctor to review my charting for accuracy before the doctor leaves the chair."

[ALCAN] A quick accuracy check prevents rework later. The framework supplies the exact habit: before the doctor leaves, ask "Just to confirm, we did DO on 3 and 14, correct?" Catch the error now, not at billing.

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

[GENERAL] What good looks like:

- The note and the odontogram always agree with each other and with what was said in the room.
- Declined or deferred treatment is recorded just as carefully as accepted treatment.
- The doctor's chart review is a five-second confirmation, because the RDA already asked about anything unclear in the moment.
- Nothing about the visit needs to be reconstructed from memory afterward.

[GENERAL] Common pitfalls:

- Guessing at a mumbled tooth number or condition instead of asking for a repeat.
- Charting the doctor's recommendation rather than the family's actual decision.
- Letting the doctor walk before the review, then chasing them down at billing time.
- Recording in the note but not the odontogram, or the reverse.

A: [ALCAN] No. Ask them to repeat it. The framework is explicit that mishearing "watch" versus "treat" changes the entire treatment plan.

A: [ALCAN] Before the doctor leaves the chair, prompted by you with a quick confirmation question.
