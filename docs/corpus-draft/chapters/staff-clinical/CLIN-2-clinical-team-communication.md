## Overview

[ALCAN] This competency is about proactively sharing relevant patient information with the clinical team, making sure any special requests, concerns, or observations made at the front desk reach the clinical staff so they can deliver excellent and timely care.

It applies to everyone in the Front Desk (DFI) role. The framework frames it simply: "You are the bridge between the front and the back. Passing critical info to the clinical team instantly ensures they are ready to give the best care possible."

## Why it matters

[ALCAN] The framework's guidance turns on one idea: information the front desk holds is worthless until it moves. "If it's not in the note, it didn't happen." Reading memos before the hello means you greet each family "with context, confidence, and fewer surprises," and writing the board is part of the definition of done: "Check-in isn't done until the Board is written."

[GENERAL] In pediatric dentistry the front desk often learns things the clinical team needs before the appointment even starts: a child's anxiety, a billing situation, a parent's specific worry from a phone call. When that context travels, the visit feels seamless to the family. When it doesn't, the family repeats themselves and the team looks disconnected.

## The moves

### Move 8: Read memos and notes before starting check-in

[ALCAN] "I always review patient memos and appointment notes prior to beginning the check in process."

[ALCAN] Read the notes before the hello. Being able to reference individual situations with grace takes your service up a notch. Check the memo for things like anxiety or billing issues before you say hello; it prevents awkward surprises and puts you one step ahead.

### Move 45: Put balances on the info board

[ALCAN] "I always inform the clinical team of existing or anticipated patient balance using the info board."

[ALCAN] A quick note on the board prevents confusion, smooths handoffs, and helps families feel every detail is handled. If you forget to write on it, the clinical team can miss crucial information. Set a mental trigger: check-in isn't done until the board is written.

### Move 79: Move VoiceStack call summaries into the patient memo

[ALCAN] "I always transfer appointment related call summaries from VoiceStack into the patient memo."

[ALCAN] Notes tell the story between calls and visits. Keep them updated so every teammate knows what has already been said and what comes next. If you struggle to transfer notes, do it while you are on the phone, not after.

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

[GENERAL] What good looks like:

- Every greeting shows the family you already know their story; nobody has to re-explain a phone call from yesterday.
- The info board and the patient memo agree with each other and with reality.
- The clinical team walks into each appointment already knowing about balances, concerns, and special requests.

[GENERAL] Common pitfalls:

- Greeting first and reading the memo after, which is when the awkward surprise happens.
- Holding information in your head or on a sticky note instead of the memo ([ALCAN] "If it's not in the note, it didn't happen").
- Finishing a call and planning to log it later; later rarely comes on a busy day.
