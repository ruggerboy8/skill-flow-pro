Role: Front Desk (DFI) · Framework code: DFI.CLIN 3 · Tagline: "Fill the schedule"

## Overview

[ALCAN] This competency is about responding effectively to same-day scheduling changes: cancellations, same-day treatment, late arrivals, and walk-ins. It means adjusting appointments promptly to accommodate changes while keeping the schedule efficient and minimizing disruption to patient care.

It applies to everyone in the Front Desk (DFI) role. The framework's framing: "Schedules change, but you stay cool! Your ability to adapt to cancellations and walk-ins keeps the team productive and ensures patients get seen."

## Why it matters

[ALCAN] The framework treats an empty chair as "a missed opportunity," and treats honesty about delays as the fix, not the failure: "A short, sincere update matters more than a perfect schedule" and "Ignoring a delay makes it worse." On emergencies, the balance is named directly: "Empathy + Boundary. You can care about their pain while being firm about the fee."

[GENERAL] For a pediatric office, schedule agility has a double payoff: open treatment slots get filled so kids get care sooner, and families in the lobby stay calm because someone acknowledged reality before they had to ask.

## The moves

### Move 10: Work the short call list daily

[ALCAN] "I always dedicate time daily to call and send a personalized text to people on the short call list, prioritizing same-day or next-day open treatment slots."

[ALCAN] Fill the day, but keep it personal. A short, genuine call or text makes families feel remembered, not marketed to, and helps more kids get care faster. If you are guessing who to call, ask the Office Manager to show you exactly how to filter the ASAP List in the software.

### Move 47: Tell waiting families when you are running behind

[ALCAN] "If we are running more than 10 minutes behind schedule, I always gently communicate that to the families in my waiting room one at a time, and assure them that we are working diligently to catch up."

[ALCAN] Let families know you see the delay, you care, and you are working to make it right. The framework's push is immediate: go into the lobby and give a 30-second update to anyone waiting. "I see you, and we're hurrying" goes a long way.

### Move 82: Absorb same-day emergencies with clear financial footing

[ALCAN] "When I receive a same-day emergency call, I adjust schedules to accommodate, verify insurance if I can, and prepare patient to pay the 'Standard Limited Exam Fee' if I cannot."

[ALCAN] Emergencies test your calm. Act fast, stay clear, and keep patients informed so the day stays under control even when it isn't. The framework suggests asking a Lead to roleplay the Limited Exam Fee conversation with you until it feels natural.

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

[GENERAL] What good looks like:

- Outreach for open slots happens every day at a protected time, not only when the schedule already looks bad.
- Calls and texts sound like a person who knows the family, not a broadcast blast.
- When the office slips behind, families hear it from you first, one at a time, before anyone has to walk up and ask.
- An emergency caller leaves the call knowing when to come in and exactly what the money side looks like.

[GENERAL] Common pitfalls:

- Sending one generic mass text and calling the list "worked."
- Making a single announcement to the whole lobby instead of the one-at-a-time, personal updates the move calls for.
- Booking an emergency without touching insurance or the fee, so the surprise lands at the desk on arrival.
- Letting fear of the fee conversation stop you from offering the appointment at all.
