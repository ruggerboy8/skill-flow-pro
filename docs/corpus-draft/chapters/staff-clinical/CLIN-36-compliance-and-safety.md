## Why it matters

[ALCAN] The one sourced rationale: reviewing Uptime tasks weekly "keeps clinical equipment safe, compliant, and ready, reducing the risk of breakdowns and safety incidents."

[GENERAL] Compliance failures are rarely dramatic on the day they start; they are missed maintenance tasks that quietly accumulate. A weekly review with a named partner (the Lead RDA) and a hard rule that every item is done or dated keeps the backlog at zero, which is the only comfortable size for a safety backlog.

## The moves

### Move 167: Weekly Uptime review with the Lead RDA

[ALCAN] "Each week, I review all Uptime tasks related to clinical equipment with my Lead RDA and ensure every item is either completed or has a clear due date before the end of that week."

[ALCAN] The standard has no third state: by end of week, every clinical equipment task in Uptime is either completed or carries a clear due date.

Related sourced context: RDA moves 26 and 128 (CLER-21) establish that RDAs check Uptime and complete outstanding tasks daily and stand ready to perform any assigned maintenance or compliance task on the Uptime checklist. This OM move is the weekly oversight layer on top of that daily execution.

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

[GENERAL] What good looks like:

- The review happens at a standing time with the Lead RDA, every week, even busy ones.
- Nothing in the clinical equipment list is ambiguous: done, or dated, with an owner.
- Recurring slips point at a system fix (task ownership, supply, scheduling) rather than a reminder.

[GENERAL] Common pitfalls:

- Skimming the list solo instead of reviewing it with the Lead RDA, losing the clinical context they bring.
- Letting "in progress" linger as an unofficial third state with no due date.
- Treating the review as complete when items are merely acknowledged rather than scheduled.
