Role: Front Desk (DFI) · Framework code: DFI.CLIN 4 · Tagline: "Know your dental codes and procedures"

## Overview

[ALCAN] This competency is about possessing basic knowledge of common dental procedures, including their typical duration, procedure code, preparation steps, and comfort considerations. The DFI provides patients with general information, answers common questions, and directs more complex inquiries to clinical staff when necessary.

It applies to everyone in the Front Desk (DFI) role. In the framework's words: "You speak the language of dentistry. Knowing your codes and procedures builds confidence with patients and makes you a huge asset to the clinical team."

## Why it matters

[ALCAN] The framework ties knowledge directly to trust and accuracy: "When you can answer confidently, families trust you and scheduling stays accurate and stress-free." It also names the patient side: "Patients fear the unknown." And on insurance: "Setting clear expectations early builds trust fast" and "Don't let insurance surprises hurt the patient relationship."

[GENERAL] A front desk that knows what a pulpotomy is, roughly how long a crown visit runs, and which claims tend to bounce can book the day realistically, answer a nervous parent in plain language, and hand harder questions to the clinical team without guessing. That is the difference between a receptionist and a Director of First Impressions.

## The moves

This competency's moves are knowledge standards ("I am able to") rather than in-the-moment behaviors.

### Move 11: Know common procedures and their durations from memory

[ALCAN] "I am able to name all common procedures and their typical duration from memory."

[ALCAN] Know the basics cold. The framework's coaching step: pick one procedure you felt unsure about today and ask a Lead to explain the basic steps to you in two minutes.

### Move 48: Know which procedures each insurance commonly denies

[ALCAN] "I am able to list the procedures that are most commonly denied by each insurance."

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

[GENERAL] What good looks like:

- A parent asks "what's a pulpotomy and how long will it take?" and gets a calm, accurate, two-sentence answer without a hold or a hallway trip.
- Appointment lengths booked at the desk match what the clinical team actually needs, because durations are known, not guessed.
- Insurance pushback is previewed at booking, so denials rarely surprise a family.
- The DFI knows exactly where their knowledge ends and hands off past that line without bluffing.

[GENERAL] Common pitfalls:

- Guessing at a clinical answer to avoid looking uninformed; a wrong answer costs more trust than a handoff.
- Learning procedure names without durations, which quietly breaks the schedule.
- Treating insurance denial patterns as billing's problem instead of a front-desk conversation tool.
