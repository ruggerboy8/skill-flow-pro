## Overview

[ALCAN] The competency definition is identical to CLIN-20: continuously centering the patient's experience throughout procedures by monitoring comfort, understanding, and emotional state, maintaining open communication, and promptly addressing signs of pain, discomfort, or fear.

For the Lead Dental Assistant, the framework adds a multiplier responsibility: the Lead is expected not only to practice these skills but to observe and coach their team on them.

## Why it matters

[GENERAL] The Lead is the person best positioned to keep patient-comfort standards real across every operatory. A monthly observation cadence means every RDA gets seen doing actual chairside work, praised for what is working, and coached on what is not, which is how "tell, show, do" and the other CLIN-20 habits stay habits instead of onboarding memories.

[ALCAN] The framework builds a small observation web around this: the Lead observes RDAs monthly (this move), and the Office Manager observes the RDA to Doctor hand-off weekly (CLIN-35).

## The moves

### Move 331: Monthly observation with feedback

[ALCAN] "I always observe each of my staff members during procedures once a month, and deliver positive and constructive feedback as appropriate."

[ALCAN] The cadence is each staff member, once a month, during procedures, with both positive and constructive feedback delivered as appropriate. The framework provides no further description, steps, or coaching text for this move.

[GENERAL] The natural rubric for these observations is the CLIN-20 move set: tell-show-do, specific positive affirmations, comfort monitoring, the patient presentation, the pre-treatment preview and video, and post-op instructions.

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

[GENERAL] What good looks like:

- Every RDA on the team is observed every month; nobody is skipped because they are experienced.
- Feedback lands close to the moment, is specific, and includes genuine praise, not only corrections.
- Patterns across observations feed the Lead's sense of what the team should practice next.

[GENERAL] Common pitfalls:

- Observing only when something has already gone wrong, which turns observation into a warning sign.
- Generic feedback ("good job in there") that changes nothing.
- Letting the monthly cadence slide during busy stretches, which is when comfort habits erode fastest.
