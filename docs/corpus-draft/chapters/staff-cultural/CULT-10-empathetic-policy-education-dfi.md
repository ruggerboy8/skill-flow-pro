# CULT-10 Empathetic Practice Policy Education (Front Desk / DFI)

> **DRAFT - AI-generated 2026-08-20 from the Pro Moves framework. Not canon. Requires review by Tim and the regional managers before entering the Ask corpus.**

## Overview

This competency is about delivering the hard-edged parts of the front desk job, cancellation consequences, balances due, lateness, portal paperwork, NPO rules, with warmth intact. The framework tagline: "Strict policies delivered with a soft hand." It belongs to the Front Desk (DFI) role; the RDA role has a smaller sibling competency (see CULT-25).

## Why it matters

[ALCAN] The framework defines it as effectively communicating and enforcing practice policies "in a firm yet empathetic manner," educating patients on requirements such as timely arrival, cancellation guidelines, paperwork completion, and rescheduling "without causing frustration or anger, ensuring understanding and cooperation."

[ALCAN] The friendly description: "You handle tough conversations with grace. Educating patients on policies without losing the connection is a superpower that builds long-term respect."

[GENERAL] Policies exist to protect the schedule and the standard of care, but a policy delivered coldly costs you the family. The skill is holding both: clear about the rule, kind about the person.

## The moves

### 1. Inform 48-hour cancellations of their consequence level (action 18)

[ALCAN] "I always firmly and kindly inform 48-hr cancellations or reschedules of their consequence level, and the next consequence, before offering one last opportunity to keep the appointment."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Policies protect the schedule, but empathy protects relationships. Explain clearly, stay kind, and end with one last chance to keep the appointment." Coaching note: "Tone is everything here. You want Firm Kindness. Read the specific Cancellation Policy script out loud twice before your next call to lock in the verbiage."

### 2. Ask "can we count on you?" when booking (action 44)

[ALCAN] "I always communicate the importance of attendance and ask whether we can count on them to be there when booking any appointment."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Turn confirmation into partnership. Framing it as 'can we count on you?' builds respect and accountability without pressure." Coaching note: "Can we count on you? is a psychological hook. If you feel awkward saying it, roleplay it with a peer today until it feels like a natural part of the conversation."

### 3. Flag outstanding balances during confirmation (action 55)

[ALCAN] "I always inform patients of outstanding balances during appointment confirmation preparing them for that balance to be collected at check in."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Be upfront before they arrive. Transparency turns a potential awkward moment into one of trust and professionalism." Coaching note: "Money conversations are hard but necessary. Don't surprise them at the desk. Practice the Pre-Call script: Just a heads up on that balance... so they arrive prepared."

### 4. Call every treatment visit the day before, review NPO, stress attendance (action 88)

[ALCAN] "Even if confirmed, I always call all treatment visits for the following day, review NPO guidelines, and communicate the importance of their attendance."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "A reminder call isn't just logistics, it's reassurance. It shows families you're invested in their visit and that every appointment matters." Coaching note: "A confirmation call is a medical safety check. If you feel rushed, pause. This call prevents the Sorry, we can't treat you conversation tomorrow."

### 5. Acknowledge lateness honestly and reset expectations (action 89)

[ALCAN] "When a patient is greater than 10 minutes late, I always communicate our understanding, then acknowledge that their wait may be more than usual."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Late arrivals happen, how you handle them matters. Be kind, stay direct, and reset expectations so the visit still feels organized and calm." Coaching note: "Resetting expectations saves the relationship. Don't just say Okay. Say: We'll squeeze you in, but there will be a wait. Be honest about the consequence."

### 6. Remind families to complete the patient portal, and warn about delays (action 154)

[ALCAN] "I always remind patients to access and complete the patient portal prior to arrival, and warn about delays if they do not."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "A gentle reminder about the portal helps families come prepared and keeps their visit running smoothly. Setting expectations early prevents delays and shows that you're looking out for their time." Coaching note: "The warning isn't a threat; it's a kindness. If you don't warn them about the delay, they will blame you when they have to wait. Say: To keep your appointment time protected, we need this done now."

## Scripts and examples

These are real script resources from the framework, lightly adapted for punctuation only.

**Cancellation consequence call** (script for action 18): "I completely understand that things come up. Because we're within our 48 hour cancellation window, we would have to count this as a late change, which would mean that for your next appointment, we wouldn't be able to schedule anything in the morning or afternoon. Is there anyone else that could possibly bring Tommy in or maybe a different time that would work for you guys to come in today?"

**Booking commitment** (script for action 44): "Ok, I've got you down for Thursday at 11:00 am with Dr. Eduardo. We've got this time set aside just for you, can we count on you to be there?"

**Balance heads-up** (script for action 55): "Just a quick heads up, you have a balance of $26 from your last visit, we'll take care of that when you check in on Thursday."

**Treatment-day confirmation call** (script for action 88): "Hi, just calling to confirm tomorrow's appointment. Please remember no food or drink after midnight so everything goes smoothly. We're looking forward to seeing you!"

**Late arrival** (script for action 89): "No worries, we understand things happen, and even though you were late, we still will have space to see you today. I'll just ask you to be patient while we find an opportunity to get you back."

**Portal reminder** (script for action 154): "If you can complete the portal forms before you come in, it keeps your visit smooth. If not, just a heads-up, it may add a few minutes at check-in."

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

[GENERAL] Good looks like: the policy is stated once, clearly, with the consequence named, and the conversation ends with an offer or a path forward, never with a scolding. Families hear the same rules the same way from every DFI.

[GENERAL] Common pitfalls: apologizing your way out of stating the consequence, so the family never actually hears the policy; leading with the rule instead of the empathy ("things come up" comes first); surprising a family with a balance at the desk instead of on the confirmation call; skipping the day-before treatment call because the appointment shows as confirmed; delivering the portal reminder without the delay warning, then absorbing the blame for the wait.
