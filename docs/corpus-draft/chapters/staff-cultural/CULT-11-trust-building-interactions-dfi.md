## Overview

Trust Building Interactions is about turning routine front desk moments, booking, check-in, review responses, into personal connection. The framework tagline: "Our patients are our friends." It belongs to the Front Desk (DFI) role; the RDA role has its own version chairside (see CULT-27).

## Why it matters

[ALCAN] The framework defines it as building "genuine connections with patients through personalized interactions," making patients "feel known and appreciated by referencing personal details and addressing their individual needs while maintaining professionalism."

[ALCAN] The friendly description: "You make people feel known, not just treated. Remembering the little details builds a bond that turns patients into raving fans."

[GENERAL] Families choose a pediatric dental home partly on clinical trust, but mostly on whether they feel seen. The front desk collects more of those small moments than anyone else in the building.

## The moves

### 1. Ask guardians their goals for the visit, and write them down (action 9)

[ALCAN] "I always ask guardians what their goals are for the visit and enter the answer into appointment notes before booking a new appointment."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Taking time to ask about goals tells families you're on their side. It's not just small talk, it's how trust begins."

[GENERAL] The second half matters as much as the first: entering the answer into appointment notes means the clinical team can pick up the thread, so the family only has to say it once.

### 2. Use preferred names, never "kiddo" or "mom" (action 46)

[ALCAN] "I always call the guardian and patient by their preferred name (not kiddo or mom)."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Names are powerful. Using them turns routine interactions into real connection, small word, big signal of respect."

### 3. Ask one curious, open-ended question at check-in (action 106)

[ALCAN] "I always ask patients at least one curious, open-ended question during the check in process."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Curiosity creates connection. One sincere question can shift a rushed check-in into a real conversation." Coaching note: "Break the script. Ask one kid today: What was the best part of your weekend? Watch how it changes the energy of the check-in."

### 4. Respond to positive Google reviews within 24 hours, personalized (action 155)

[ALCAN] "I always apply the Alcan template to write a personalized response to all positive Google reviews within 24 hrs."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Responding to positive reviews quickly shows gratitude and strengthens relationships. Personalizing each message makes families feel recognized and reinforces what they loved about their visit." Coaching note: "Speed matters. A response 3 days later looks automated. Copy the Alcan Template to a sticky note on your desktop so you can paste-and-personalize in 30 seconds."

## Scripts and examples

Sourced scripts from the framework, lightly adapted for punctuation only.

**Goals question** (script for action 9): "Before we see you, is there anything specific you'd like the doctor to focus on for this visit?"

**Curious check-in question** (script for action 106): "Hi there! I love your outfit today. What did you do over the weekend?"

[GENERAL] Preferred-name practice: when a new family checks in, confirm it out loud once, "And do you go by Alexandra, or something shorter?", then use that name for the rest of the interaction and note it for next time.

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

[GENERAL] Good looks like: the family's stated goal shows up in the appointment notes and the clinical team references it; kids get greeted by their own name; every check-in includes at least one moment of genuine, non-dental conversation; review responses go out same day and mention something specific from the review.

[GENERAL] Common pitfalls: asking the goals question but not writing the answer down, so the family repeats themselves in the operatory; defaulting to "mom," "dad," or "kiddo" under time pressure; asking yes-or-no questions instead of open-ended ones; sending review responses that read as copy-paste because the personalization step got skipped.
