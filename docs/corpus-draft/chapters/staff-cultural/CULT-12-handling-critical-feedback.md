# CULT-12 Handling Critical Feedback (Front Desk / DFI)

> **DRAFT - AI-generated 2026-08-20 from the Pro Moves framework. Not canon. Requires review by Tim and the regional managers before entering the Ask corpus.**

## Overview

This competency covers what a DFI does when a guardian is upset: staying calm, restating what was heard, owning the practice's part, and making sure the conversation ends with a next step. The framework tagline: "Defuse & improve." The RDA role has a parallel competency (CULT-28) with the same spine.

## Why it matters

[ALCAN] The framework defines it as responding "to patient complaints or negative feedback calmly and professionally," addressing concerns appropriately and "seeking mutually beneficial solutions to enhance patient satisfaction and improve practice services."

[ALCAN] The friendly description: "Feedback is an opportunity, and you handle it like a pro. Turning a complaint into a solution shows maturity and deep care for the patient experience."

[GENERAL] The front desk absorbs most complaints first, and how the first sixty seconds go usually decides whether the issue ends at the desk or becomes a one-star review. The three moves here form a sequence: restate, own, resolve.

## The moves

### 1. Stay calm and restate the feedback first (action 14)

[ALCAN] "When presented with critical feedback, I always remain calm and first restate the guardian's feedback."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Take a breath before you speak. Restate what you heard to show you understand, it turns feedback from a hit to a handshake." Coaching note: "Validation lowers the temperature. Practice saying: I want to make sure I hear you correctly. You're saying [X]? Ask a Lead to roleplay this with you for 60 seconds."

### 2. Acknowledge your part, or ours (action 51)

[ALCAN] "When presented with critical feedback, I actively acknowledge my personal or our collective role in the situation."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Teams grow when people own their part. Admit what's true, skip the spin, and help fix it fast." Coaching note: "Defensiveness kills trust. Try the Two Words ownership trick: I apologize. Ask the Office Manager how they handle valid complaints without over-promising."

### 3. Do not end the conversation without a next step (action 109)

[ALCAN] "When presented with critical feedback, I always ensure that a next-step or resolution has been found before finishing the interaction."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Don't end a feedback talk without a plan. We don't want to walk away without knowing at least a next step, even if it's just coordinating a chat with a manager." Coaching note: "Don't let them leave angry. Before they go, ask: Do you feel like we have a plan to fix this? If they say no, get the Manager."

## Scripts and examples

Sourced scripts from the framework, lightly adapted for punctuation only.

**Restating** (script for action 14): "Thanks for sharing that. It sounds like you're frustrated because you weren't expecting to have an outstanding balance today, is that right?"

**Owning your part** (script for action 51): "You're right, I could've double-checked that earlier. I'll make sure it's corrected moving forward."

[GENERAL] Closing with a next step: "Here's what I'd like to do: I'll flag this for our office manager today, and one of us will call you by tomorrow with an answer. Does that work for you?"

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

[GENERAL] Good looks like: the guardian hears their own complaint reflected back accurately before anyone explains anything; ownership is stated plainly without excuses or over-promising; every complaint conversation ends with a named next step, and the family agrees it is a plan. Escalation to the Office Manager is treated as good practice, not failure. Per CULT-42, the Office Manager personally follows up on escalated concerns before the end of the next day.

[GENERAL] Common pitfalls: explaining or defending before restating; the non-apology ("I'm sorry you feel that way") instead of real ownership; promising a specific remedy you cannot guarantee; letting a visibly unhappy family walk out without asking whether they feel there is a plan; taking the complaint personally and matching the guardian's energy.
