## Overview

The RDA's slice of policy education is small but high-leverage: reinforcing the importance of the next appointment at the chair, and asking families for a Google review on the way out. The framework tagline: "Rules with care." The fuller policy-conversation toolkit lives with the front desk in CULT-10.

## Why it matters

[ALCAN] The framework defines it as effectively communicating and enforcing practice policies "in a firm yet empathetic manner," educating patients on procedures like timely check-in, appointment arrivals, cancellations, and rescheduling "without causing frustration or anger, ensuring understanding and cooperation."

[ALCAN] The friendly description: "You guide patients with kindness. Helping them understand our policies prevents frustration and keeps the relationship positive."

[GENERAL] Families weigh what the clinical team says differently than what the desk says. When the person who just cared for their child says the next visit matters, it lands.

## The moves

### 1. Reinforce the next appointment and the consequences of missing it (action 30)

[ALCAN] "After scheduling the next appointment, I always verbally emphasize the importance keeping that appointment and the consequences for missing it."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "A brief reminder sets expectations early. Emphasizing the importance helps families commit to care and prevents unnecessary delays in treatment."

### 2. Ask every family for a Google review, with the QR code (action 67)

[ALCAN] "Except in extreme cases, I always request that all families leave us a Google review after their appointment, and direct them to the QR code to scan."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Google reviews are a huge part of families discovering our excellent care. We ask for them to help make sure other people can find us and get great oral healthcare." Coaching note: "Compliments don't count; reviews do. If they say You guys are great, reply: The best way to thank us is scanning this QR code."

## Scripts and examples

Sourced scripts from the framework, lightly adapted for punctuation only.

**Next-appointment emphasis** (script for action 30): "Ok, so we've got that scheduled for Tuesday the 4th of next month. It's really important to keep that appointment in order for us to stay on track for your child's oral health."

**Review ask** (script for action 67): "I hope you had a positive experience today. We're really proud of the care we offer, and I wonder if you'd be willing to leave us a Google review so that other families like yours can find us and get the same great care. I have a QR code right here that you can scan to make it super easy."

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

[GENERAL] Good looks like: every dismissal includes a warm, specific mention of why the next visit matters for this child; the review ask is confident and paired with the QR code so it takes ten seconds; a spontaneous compliment gets redirected into a review on the spot.

[GENERAL] Common pitfalls: mumbling past the consequences part because it feels awkward; asking for a review from a family whose visit clearly went badly (that is the "extreme cases" exception); mentioning the review but not having the QR code at hand, which drops the conversion to near zero.
