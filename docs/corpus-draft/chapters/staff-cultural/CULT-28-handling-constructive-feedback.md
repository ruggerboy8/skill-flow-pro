## Overview

The RDA's version of complaint handling: stay calm and restate, invite more with "tell me more," and close with a next step. The framework tagline: "Turn complaints into wins." It mirrors the DFI's CULT-12 sequence, adapted for the chairside setting where the complaint often lands mid-visit.

## Why it matters

[ALCAN] The framework defines it as responding "to patient complaints or negative feedback calmly and professionally," addressing concerns appropriately and "seeking mutually beneficial solutions to enhance patient satisfaction and improve practice services."

[ALCAN] The friendly description: "You listen to understand. Responding to concerns with calm professionalism turns a moment of friction into a moment of connection."

[GENERAL] Chairside complaints are different from desk complaints: the child is often present, the visit is still in progress, and the doctor may need to know before it ends. The three moves give the RDA a sequence that works inside those constraints.

## The moves

### 1. Stay calm and restate the complaint first (action 32)

[ALCAN] "When responding to frustration, I always remain calm and first attempt to restate the guardian's complaint."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Starting with calmness keeps tough conversations productive. Restating what you heard shows that you're listening and takes the tension out of the moment."

### 2. Then say "tell me more" and actually listen (action 69)

[ALCAN] "After restating critical feedback, I always use the phrase 'tell me more' and actively listen to the response."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "'Tell me more' opens the door for clarity. It signals that you're not defending, you're understanding. Active listening helps families feel heard and respected." Coaching note: "Curiosity cures conflict. If a parent is upset, lean in. Tell me more about that. Let them vent before you solve."

### 3. Close with a next step or resolution (action 132)

[ALCAN] "When presented with critical feedback or conflict from a parent or guardian, I always ensure that a next-step or resolution has been found before finishing the interaction."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Every piece of feedback needs a clear finish. Ending with a concrete next step shows professionalism and turns criticism into forward motion."

## Scripts and examples

Sourced scripts from the framework, lightly adapted for punctuation only.

**Restating** (script for action 32): "So what I'm hearing is that the wait today felt longer than you expected, and that made things stressful, is that right?"

**Tell me more** (script for action 69): "I can see that you're frustrated, can you tell me more about why?"

**Next step** (script for action 132): "Here's what I'll do next: I'll update the doctor about your concern and make sure we adjust the plan so this doesn't happen again."

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

[GENERAL] Good looks like: the sequence runs in order, restate, invite more, resolve, and the parent visibly softens between step one and step three; the doctor hears about the concern before the family leaves; the child never becomes the audience for the conflict.

[GENERAL] Common pitfalls: jumping straight to fixing before the parent feels heard; using "tell me more" as a formality and interrupting the answer; ending the visit with the concern acknowledged but no named next step; debating clinical judgments with the parent instead of looping in the doctor.
