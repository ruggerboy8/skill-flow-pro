## Why it matters

[ALCAN] The framework's friendly description: "You set clear expectations, run effective huddles and 1:1s, give real-time feedback, and coach your team toward both growth and results."

[GENERAL] An office manager's presence is a coaching instrument. Teams calibrate to what their manager notices, and a manager who is visible on the floor twice a day catches small problems while they are still small and reinforces good work while it is still fresh.

## The moves

### 1. Walk the floor twice daily (action 180)

[ALCAN] "I always walk the floor twice daily to check in briefly with every team member, address any immediate barriers, and give specific encouragement when I see something done well."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Being visibly present and supportive on the floor builds morale, surfaces issues early, and reinforces positive behaviors in real time." Practical tip from the framework: "Set two daily reminders to step out of your office, walk the floor, and connect with each team member."

[GENERAL] The move has three parts, and the third is the one most often skipped: encouragement that is specific ("the way you reset expectations with that late family was exactly right") rather than generic ("good job today").

## Scripts and examples

No script resources exist in the framework for this competency.

[GENERAL] A floor-walk check-in: "Anything in your way right now that I can clear?" followed by actually clearing it, or naming when you will.

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

[GENERAL] Good looks like: both walks happen even on chaotic days; every team member gets a brief personal touch, not just the ones near the office door; barriers raised on the walk get a same-day response; praise names the specific behavior.

[GENERAL] Common pitfalls: the walk becoming an inspection instead of a check-in; only surfacing on the floor when something is wrong, which teaches the team that your presence means trouble; collecting barriers and never closing the loop.
