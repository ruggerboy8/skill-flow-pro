## Overview

The Office Manager's trust competency centers on follow-through with families, especially after something has gone wrong. The framework tagline: "Earn it through follow-through." This is a thin section in the source data: one pro move, no script or prose resources, so this chapter is mostly a stub with gaps.

## Why it matters

[ALCAN] The framework's friendly description: "You build trust with families by doing what you say, explaining things clearly, and handling problems with transparency and care."

[GENERAL] When a complaint reaches the office manager, the family is already once disappointed. The manager's follow-up is the practice's second first impression, and speed plus clarity is what rebuilds the trust the original problem spent.

## The moves

### 1. Personally follow up on every escalated concern by end of next day (action 181)

[ALCAN] "I always personally follow up with any parent concern or complaint escalated to me before the end of the next day, clearly summarizing what we'll do next and how they can reach me if necessary."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Prompt, personal follow-up on escalated concerns shows parents that their experience matters and helps rebuild trust after a problem." Practical tip from the framework: "When a concern is escalated, note it immediately and schedule time to call or speak with that parent before the next day ends."

[GENERAL] Notice the three commitments packed into the move: personal (not delegated), deadlined (end of next day), and concrete (a summary of what happens next plus a direct line back to you). This is the closing half of the desk-level and chairside feedback moves in CULT-12 and CULT-28, which end by promising a next step; this move is where that promise gets kept.

[GENERAL] A follow-up call shape: "Hi, this is [name], the office manager at [location]. I heard about what happened with the wait yesterday and I wanted to reach out personally. Here's what we're doing about it... And if anything else comes up, you can reach me directly at the office."

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

[GENERAL] Good looks like: every escalated concern gets logged when it arrives and personally answered within the deadline; the family leaves the call knowing exactly what happens next and how to reach you; the team members who escalated hear back about the outcome, so escalating feels worthwhile.

[GENERAL] Common pitfalls: delegating the callback, which quietly breaks the "personally" in the move; calling without a plan, so the conversation re-opens the wound without closing anything; resolving the family's issue but never fixing the underlying process that caused it.
