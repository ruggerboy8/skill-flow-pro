## Overview

The Office Manager's emotional intelligence competency is about managing the office's mood, not just their own. The framework tagline: "Stay steady, read the room." This is a thin section in the source data: one pro move, no script or prose resources, so this chapter is mostly a stub with gaps. The RDA-level counterpart is CULT-26.

## Why it matters

[ALCAN] The framework's friendly description: "You stay calm under pressure, pick up on emotional cues from staff and parents, and respond in ways that de-escalate and stabilize."

[GENERAL] Mood is contagious, and in a pediatric office it flows downhill fast: a tense team makes tense parents, and tense parents make frightened kids. The manager is the one person positioned to notice the office-wide temperature and change it on purpose.

## The moves

### 1. Track the office mood all day, and intervene when it slips (action 182)

[ALCAN] "I always stay aware of the overall mood in the office throughout the day, I pause what I'm doing as necessary to either reset the tone with encouragement and clarity or address the source of tension directly with the team."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Managing the overall mood in the office keeps stress from spreading and helps the team stay calm, focused, and patient-centered." Practical tip from the framework: "When you feel the energy dip or spike, take a moment to name it, reset expectations, and offer calm, clear guidance."

[GENERAL] The move offers two distinct responses, and choosing the right one is the skill: a tone reset (encouragement and clarity aimed at everyone) when the mood has drifted, versus a direct conversation when there is an identifiable source of tension. Naming the energy out loud, as the tip suggests, is often the reset by itself.

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

[GENERAL] Good looks like: the manager notices a mood shift before anyone reports it; interventions are calm and specific rather than cheerleading; sources of tension get addressed directly and privately, not managed around for weeks; the manager's own steadiness under pressure is the loudest signal in the room.

[GENERAL] Common pitfalls: staying heads-down in the office during exactly the stretches when the floor needs a reset; broadcasting your own stress and then asking the team to stay calm; treating tension between two team members as weather instead of something to address.
