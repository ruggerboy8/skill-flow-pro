## Why it matters

[ALCAN] The framework's friendly description: "You support org-level marketing, help the team show up at community events, and nurture relationships with referral partners."

[GENERAL] For a pediatric practice, most new families arrive through some mix of online presence and word of mouth. A steady weekly stream of authentic office content is the cheapest marketing the practice has, and it only happens if someone owns the cadence. That someone is the office manager.

## The moves

### 1. Confirm the week's social content is gathered and submitted (action 183)

[ALCAN] "I always confirm that our assigned social media content for the week (photos, videos, or stories) has been gathered from the team and submitted to the marketing contact by the end of the last clinic day of the week."

[ALCAN] Framework guidance: "Consistently submitting social content keeps your community presence active and shows families what makes your practice special." Practical tip from the framework: "Choose a specific day each week to collect content from the team and send it to marketing before the day ends."

[GENERAL] The verb in the move is "confirm," not "create." The manager's job is the cadence: content is assigned, gathered from the team through the week, and handed off on time, every week, without a marketing person having to chase it.

## Scripts and examples

No script resources exist in the framework for this competency.

[GENERAL] A weekly nudge to the team: "Reminder that Friday is content day. If you catch a great moment this week, a proud patient, a fun team photo, send it my way so it makes this week's batch."

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

[GENERAL] Good looks like: content goes out on schedule every single week; the whole team knows what kind of moments to capture, so the manager curates rather than scrambles; the content shows real people and real moments from the office.

[GENERAL] Common pitfalls: treating the deadline as flexible, which turns a weekly rhythm into a monthly apology; the manager producing all the content alone instead of gathering it from the team; capturing patients without whatever consent the practice requires.
