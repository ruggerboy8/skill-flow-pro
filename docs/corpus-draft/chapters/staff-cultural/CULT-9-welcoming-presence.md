# CULT-9 Welcoming Presence (Front Desk / DFI)

## Overview

Welcoming Presence is the first Cultural competency for the Front Desk role, which Alcan calls the Director of First Impressions (DFI). Its tagline in the framework is "Unreasonable hospitality." This competency covers how a DFI shows up: the greeting, the tone of voice on the phone, the small hospitality gestures, and the professional appearance that together set the emotional temperature of the whole visit.

Applies to: Front Desk (DFI) team members at every Alcan location.

## Why it matters

[ALCAN] The framework describes this competency as consistently maintaining "a positive, calm, and welcoming demeanor, providing a reassuring and confident presence for patients both in person and over the phone," and enhancing the patient experience "by making everyone feel comfortable and valued from the moment they arrive."

[ALCAN] The framework's friendly description puts it this way: "You are the warmth of the practice. Your calm, welcoming presence can turn a nervous patient into a relaxed one the moment they walk in."

[GENERAL] In pediatric dentistry the stakes are higher than in most service settings. Many children arrive anxious, and many parents arrive braced for a struggle. The first thirty seconds at the desk often decide whether the visit starts calm or starts tense, and the clinical team inherits whichever one you hand them.

## The moves

### 1. Stand up and greet every patient with a smile (action 1)

[ALCAN] "I always stand up and greet every patient and their guardian with a smile."

[ALCAN] From the framework guidance: "Stand, smile, make eye contact. That moment says: 'We're glad you're here.' It costs nothing and changes everything." The coaching note adds: "Standing up commands respect and shows warmth. If your legs are tired, at least lean forward and make direct eye contact. Do not greet the computer screen."

[GENERAL] The habit to build is greeting the person before touching the keyboard. Whatever you were typing can wait three seconds.

### 2. Genuinely offer coffee or water after check-in (action 37)

[ALCAN] "I always genuinely offer coffee or water to guardians after check in is complete."

[ALCAN] From the framework guidance: "Offer something simple, a coffee, a water, a smile. Those few seconds turn a transaction into a welcome." The coaching note is blunt: "Hospitality disrupts anxiety. If you feel too busy to offer water, you are too busy. Pause, breathe, and make the offer."

[GENERAL] "Genuinely" is the operative word. A mumbled offer while looking at the screen reads as a formality. Make eye contact and mean it.

### 3. Dress in business casual, even on non-patient days (action 108)

[ALCAN] "As part of the administrative team, I always dress in business casual attire when I am at work, even on non-patient days."

[ALCAN] From the framework guidance: "The way you show up changes the energy of the whole office. Professional doesn't mean fancy, it means showing pride in your work every day." The coaching note: "Your look signals authority. If you're unsure about an outfit, check the Culture Guide or simply ask a Lead DFI: Is this client-ready?"

### 4. Center yourself and smile before answering the phone (action 149)

[ALCAN] "I always center myself and smile in real life before answering the phone, ensuring a warm tone of voice."

[ALCAN] From the framework guidance: "Families can hear your smile. Taking a breath before answering helps you sound warm and steady, turning every phone call into a positive first impression." The coaching note: "You can hear a smile. If you're stressed, the caller hears Busy. Take one breath, physically smile, then pick up. It hacks your brain into warmth."

## Scripts and examples

The framework carries no script resources for these four moves, so the examples below are drafted, not sourced.

[GENERAL] Greeting a family at the door: "Good morning! Welcome in. You must be here with Maya, we've been looking forward to seeing you."

[GENERAL] The hospitality offer: "While you get settled, can I grab you a coffee or a water?"

[GENERAL] Answering the phone: pause, breathe, smile, then: "Thank you for calling, this is [name], how can I help you today?"

## What good looks like / common pitfalls

[GENERAL] Good looks like: every arriving family is greeted by a standing, smiling person within seconds, before any paperwork talk. The phone voice matches the in-person voice. The hospitality offer happens even on the busiest days, because that is exactly when families feel most rushed.

[GENERAL] Common pitfalls: greeting the screen instead of the person; letting a stressful call bleed into the next one; skipping the coffee-or-water offer when the lobby is full; treating non-patient days as an excuse to dress down; a warm in-person presence paired with a flat, hurried phone tone.
