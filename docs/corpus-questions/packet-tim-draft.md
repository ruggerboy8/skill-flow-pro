## Part 1: Two 10-minute talk-throughs

### 1. New-hire basics, answered like it is their first Monday

Nothing in the current material tells a new hire the ordinary things, and those
are the questions they ask most in week one.

**Talk through:** What time do people arrive? How do you ask for a day off?
Which holidays are we closed? How do you call in sick, as staff and separately
as a doctor? What do people wear, by role? And who does a new hire meet in
their first week? If the employee handbook already covers any of this, just say
"handbook covers that" and keep moving.

**What good looks like:** concrete and specific, like you are talking to
someone who starts Monday morning. "Be in the building by 7:45 because huddle
starts at 7:50" beats "arrive on time."

### 2. The cancellation ladder, said the way a DFI should say it

Cancellation consequences are a scored Pro Move for both the front desk and
RDAs, but the 2022 policy document and the 2024 phone scripts describe two
different ladders, so right now nobody can state the real one.

**Talk through:** A family cancels inside 48 hours or no-shows. What happens
the first time? The second? The third? What exactly does each level restrict?
Is arriving 15 minutes late still an automatic reschedule everywhere? And when
a family gets dismissed from the practice for chronic no-shows, whose call is
that? For reference, the 2022 version said: first offense restricts rebooking
to midday slots for 12 months, second is same-day standby only, third ends the
relationship with 30 days of emergency coverage. The 2024 scripts say: courtesy
reschedule first, 24-hour scheduling list second.

**What good looks like:** the ladder as you would want a DFI to say it on the
phone, kindly, with the actual consequences attached.
