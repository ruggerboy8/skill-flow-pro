# Baseline Review: session template and facilitation guide

Two pieces, and they are used differently:

- **Part 1** is the agenda itself. Paste it into the Meeting Agenda editor in
  the app, then hit **Save** to keep it as your reusable template (the
  **Load** button pulls it back for every future baseline review). Remember
  the agenda is published to the doctor: they read it in their prep. Keep it
  doctor-appropriate.
- **Part 2** is for you only. It never goes in the app. Print it, keep it on
  a second screen, or read it once before your first few conversations.

Target length for the meeting: 45 to 60 minutes.

---

## Part 1: The agenda (paste this into the app)

**Your Baseline Review**

This is our starting conversation. It is not an evaluation. The point is to
get on the same page about where you are today and to pick a small number of
things worth your attention over the next few months.

Here is how we will spend the time:

1. **How this works** (5 min). What this coaching track is, how often we will
   meet, and what happens with what we discuss.
2. **Your read** (10 min). You walk me through your self-assessment. What
   stood out, what felt easy to rate, what you were unsure about.
3. **What I have seen** (10 min). I will share what I have observed working
   alongside you, starting with what is already strong.
4. **Where we focus** (10 min). We each bring one Pro Move to the
   conversation. We will land together on what matters most right now.
5. **What you will try** (10 min). We agree on a small number of specific
   things to practice before we meet again. Three at the very most.
6. **Next steps** (5 min). We book the next conversation and you tell me what
   would make these more useful.

**Before we meet:** finish your prep in ProMoves, pick the one Pro Move you
most want to talk about, and jot down anything else on your mind. Wins,
frustrations, questions, all of it is fair game.

## Part 2: Facilitation guide (coach only)

### Before the meeting

- Finish your own observed baseline first. The app will hold the Baseline
  Review Prep screen until you do.
- Open Baseline Review Prep and read the ranked gaps. Pick the one or two you
  would actually raise. Not all of them.
- Have one specific, real example ready for each thing you plan to name. A
  moment you actually witnessed beats a rating every time.

### 1. Frame it (5 min)

**Purpose:** lower the stakes before anything else happens.

Say out loud:
- This is a recurring conversation, not a performance review.
- Roughly how often you will meet (every two to four weeks is the goal).
- That you completed your own version of the assessment, and that it is how
  you prepared, not a grade you are handing them.

**Avoid:** opening with your observations. Avoid apologizing for the process
or hedging so much that they wonder what is coming.

### 2. Their read first (10 min)

**Purpose:** they speak before you do. If you take one thing from this guide,
take this one. Whoever talks first sets the frame, and it should be them.

Ask:
- "What was that like to go through?"
- "Anything you rated where you hesitated?"
- "Where do you feel most solid right now? Where do you feel least sure?"
- If they flagged a domain in their gut check: "You flagged this one. Say
  more about that."

**Avoid:** correcting their self-assessment. You are not fact-checking, you
are listening.

**Good looks like:** they are doing most of the talking in this block.

### 3. Your observations (10 min)

**Purpose:** ground the conversation in what you have actually seen.

Start with strengths, and be specific. Not "you are great with parents" but
"the way you slowed down with the mom on Tuesday who was anxious about
sedation." Specific praise is believable. General praise is noise.

Then the growth areas:
- **Name the Pro Move, not the number you gave it.** The number invites
  debate. The behavior invites conversation.
- Where they rated themselves high and you saw something different, ask
  before you assert: "How do you think that one goes on your busiest day?"
- Where they rated themselves low and you see real strength, say so plainly.
  Newer doctors routinely underrate themselves, and correcting that is just
  as valuable as raising a gap.

**Avoid:** reading your ratings aloud. Avoid stacking more than two or three
growth observations. A long list does not land, it just deflates.

### 4. Choose the focus (10 min)

**Purpose:** converge, together.

You each brought a Pro Move into this meeting. Put both on the table. If they
overlap, that is a gift, say so. If they differ, that difference is the most
interesting thing in the room.

Ask: "Of everything we have talked about, what would make the biggest
difference to your day if it got easier?"

Land on one or two. Not five. Their pick should carry at least as much weight
as yours.

### 5. Action steps (10 min)

**Purpose:** turn the conversation into something they can actually do on
Monday.

- Three at the very most. For a first conversation, one or two is often
  better.
- Concrete enough to picture. "Be better at case presentation" is not an
  action step. "Ask what matters most to you before presenting treatment, on
  every new patient exam this month" is.
- Let them say it in their own words, then write it down that way.
- Ask: "What would get in the way of that?" The answer is usually the real
  conversation.

### 6. Close (5 min)

- Book the next conversation, or at least agree when you will book it. The
  app will ask you for that date when you write up the meeting.
- Tell them what happens next: you will send a summary, they review and
  confirm it.
- Ask for feedback on the conversation itself: "Was that useful? Anything you
  would want different next time?" Asking this in the first meeting sets the
  tone for every one after it.

### After the meeting (10 minutes, same day)

Write it up while it is fresh:

1. **Summary.** Warm, second person, what you discussed and agreed on.
2. **Action steps.** Up to three. Tag each one to the Pro Move it relates to.
   If the conversation branched somewhere unexpected, take a quick look for a
   Pro Move that fits before you skip the tag.
3. **Next meeting date**, if you have it.
4. **The thirty-second self-check.** Five sliders, private to you. Worth
   doing honestly.

### Common traps

- Talking more than they do.
- Turning it into a list of everything you have ever noticed.
- Softening so much that no real observation actually lands.
- Agreeing to five action steps, which is the same as agreeing to none.
- Ending the meeting without a date or a next step.
